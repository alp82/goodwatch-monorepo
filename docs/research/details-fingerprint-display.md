# Showing a multi-dimensional profile compactly on a details page

Research for the GoodWatch details-page fingerprint. Users report that it takes up a lot of space and that it is hard to get useful information out of it. This document covers how other sites, in film and in other fields, show profile-like data about one item, and ends with reusable patterns and concrete directions.

Date: 2026-10-17. Method: each site's own pages (WebFetch, or curl with a browser user agent), official help, docs and blog posts, the companies' own APIs, and Nielsen Norman Group and data-visualisation sources. **Many sites block automated fetching** (IMDb, Fragrantica, StoryGraph, RYM, AllMusic, Booking, Vivino taste block, DoesTheDogDie and others). Those claims rest on official help pages or press coverage and are marked **[not verified first-hand]**. Unless a source is given, space estimates are inferences, not measurements.

## 1. What GoodWatch has today

### 1.1 Data available on the details page

The details payload carries `TitleFingerprint`: `scores`, `highlightKeys`, `essenceTags`, `pillars`, `socialSuitability[].name`, `viewingContext[].name` (`goodwatch-webapp/app/server/types/details-types.tsx`). The full analysis also has `essenceText` and `content_advisories`, but the payload drops them (`server/title-details-shape.ts`: "No consumer reads the essence text…").

| Data | Shape | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Attribute scores | about 80 keys, 0–10, in 5 groups: emotional palette (13), humor (7), themes/world (20), cognitive/structural (19), aesthetic/production (15) | `server/utils/fingerprint.ts` `CoreScores` |
| Pillars | 6 aggregates (Energy, Heart, Humor, World, Craft, Style), each a tier from 0 to 4. Each uses its own aggregation (top-2 mean, median, RMS) over a subset of keys | `computePillarScores` |
| Highlight keys | the LLM's chosen standout attributes | `DNAAnalysis.highlightKeys` |
| Essence tags | short descriptive tags, currently rendered as badges in About | `ui/details/About.tsx` |
| Social suitability | 11 booleans (Adults, Date Night, Family, Friends, Party, Intergenerational, Kids, Partner, Public-safe, Solo, Teens) | `SOCIAL_SUITABILITY_REGISTRY` |
| Viewing context | 6 booleans (Background, Binge, Comfort, Drop-in, Escapism, Thought-provoking) | `VIEWING_CONTEXT_REGISTRY` |
| Text | template sentences built from scores: `pillarLine` headline, `pillarBrief` verdict and note per pillar, `pillarSentence`, `skipIf`, `matchingMoods` | `ui/fingerprint/fingerprintText.ts` |
| Unused components | `GenreMix` (top-3 subgenre stacked bar), `Badges`, legacy `Pillars` block-meter | `ui/fingerprint/*` |

### 1.2 Current layout (`ui/details/DetailsFingerprint.tsx`)

- Heading, then a headline in text-3xl (for example "Relentless energy, masterful craft.").
- Six full-width rows, one per pillar, each `py-6`. A row holds the name and emoji, a 4-segment meter, a verdict in text-lg plus an optional note, and three "driver" bars with numbers. Below `lg` these stack vertically and add a "See all traits" button.
- Selecting a row swaps all six rows for a pillar detail panel: back button, pillar tabs, a big verdict in text-4xl, prose, "more like this" links, and every attribute bar, with descriptions.
- "Who it's for", "Skip it if" and "Find more like it" chip groups.
- A footnote: "our scores for 70+ attributes".

**Rough space cost (inferred from the classes):** each desktop row is about 110–130 px, so roughly 700–800 px for the rows. With the heading and the audience block the section is about 1,000 px. On phones each row stacks to about 200 px or more, so the section probably runs past 1,500 px. The default view shows 6 meters, 6 verdict sentences, up to 6 notes and 18 bars with numbers. That is about 30 separate readings before the reader learns "is this for me?". This is far denser than any default view found in this research (see §8).

## 2. Perception and UX research to anchor the choices

| Finding | Implication | Source |
|---|---|---|
| People read **length and 2D position** most accurately; area and angle are hard to compare; colour has no perceived order, so it should not encode magnitude. NN/g groups pie charts, gauges and **radar charts** as encodings that "do not convey well quantitative relationships". | Use bars or dots on an aligned scale, not radar charts, pies or gauges. Colour should mark category (pillar), not amount. | [NN/g, Dashboards: preattentive](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/) |
| Cleveland and McGill's ranking: position on a common scale is best, then length, then angle and slope, then area, then colour saturation or hue last. | Bars that share one baseline beat anything radial. | [Cornell INFO 3312 summary](https://info3312.infosci.cornell.edu/slides/04-graphical-perception.html), [FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/2010/03/20/graphical-perception-learn-the-fundamentals-first) |
| In a radar chart only the distance from the centre is data. Category order is an editorial choice that changes the shape, and the connecting lines imply a progression that isn't there. Suggested alternatives are radial column or lollipop charts, small multiples, or plain bars. | A pillar radar would look like a "fingerprint", but the shape would be arbitrary. If a glyph is wanted, a radial bar ("stellar") chart with a fixed order is safer. | [Significance: Off the radar](https://significancemagazine.com/off-the-radar-here-are-some-alternatives/), [Observable: avoid radar charts](https://observablehq.com/blog/avoid-radar-charts) (fetch returned 429; title and thesis from search results) |
| Progressive disclosure: "initially, show users only a few of the most important options". Make the path to the rest obvious and well labelled. More than two disclosure levels "typically have low usability". | Two levels at most: summary, then full breakdown. The current overview, then pillar, then per-attribute descriptions is effectively three. | [NN/g, Progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) |
| Accordions help when users need only a few pieces of information, and hurt when they must compare across panels. "Easy access to essential information matters more than reduced page length." | Keep the essentials visible. Hide only the long tail, and don't force a reader to open six panels to compare pillars. | [NN/g, Accordions on desktop](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accordions-on-desktop/) |
| Inverted pyramid: the conclusion first, so skimmers who stop after one sentence still get it. | Lead with one sentence or tag line, not with a grid. | [NN/g, Inverted pyramid](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/inverted-pyramid/) |
| Tag clouds: "most normal users don't know what they are". Word clouds are worse than a one-paragraph summary. Research says clouds are scanned, not read, and are poor for finding a specific term. | Use a ranked list of chips (order carries the weight), not a cloud with sized fonts. | [NN/g, Tag cloud examples](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tag-cloud-examples/), [Wikipedia: Tag cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tag_cloud) |
| Baymard: users rely more on ratings distribution summaries than on individual reviews. | A small distribution or summary visual earns its space. Lengthy per-item text does not. | [Baymard, product page research](https://baymard.com/product-page) (details paywalled; headline only) |
| Bullet graphs (Stephen Few) are compact linear replacements for gauges. | A single thin bar with a reference tick ("typical for drama") is a compact way to show one score in context. | [NN/g dashboards article](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/) (recommends linear bullet charts over gauges) |

## 8. Cross-site observations

1. **Default views are tiny.** Across about 40 sites the default is one verdict word or number, one sentence and 3–9 tags or bars. The largest default grids are Common Sense Media (7 rows of words) and Steam (20 unscored chips). GoodWatch shows about 30 readings by default.
2. **Words carry magnitude better than numbers for lay readers.** Common Sense Media ("a lot / some / very little"), IMDb (None–Severe), ESRB ("Mild"), Steam ("Very Positive"), Booking ("Good") and AllTrails (Easy–Strenuous) all keep a number internally and show a word. GoodWatch's verdicts already do this, but they sit next to meters and numbered bars that repeat the same information.
3. **Order is the cheapest way to show weight.** Steam, RYM, Goodreads and Last.fm rank their chips and show no numbers. AniList adds a % only because its tags have a measured strength.
4. **Show what stands out; omit what is absent.** Common Sense Media leaves empty dots blank, Netflix shows 2–3 tags, Pandora showed 3–5 of about 400 traits, and Flaviar caps at 9. Nobody shows "Humor: none" as a full row unless the absence is the point ("Serious throughout" is useful; "Thin world" is not).
5. **Closed questions read faster than scores.** StoryGraph's "plot- or character-driven?", DoesTheDogDie's yes/no list, HLTB's "how long if I just do the story?" tiles and BGG's "best at 4 players". GoodWatch's suitability and context booleans are already answers to questions ("Good for date night?"), but they currently appear only as unlabelled chips at the bottom.
6. **Use bipolar scales only for real opposites.** Vivino's light–bold works because the poles exclude each other. GoodWatch has a few natural pairs: `slow_burn` vs. `fast_pace`, `hopefulness` vs. `bleakness`, `contemporary_realism` vs. `fantasy`, and roughly `dialogue_centrality` vs. `spectacle`.
7. **Put the evidence one tap away, not a whole second interface.** RT (click the score), BGG (click the fact), Amazon (tap the chip), RTINGS (hover the usage score), IMDb (a subpage). GoodWatch's row-to-panel swap replaces the whole section instead.
8. **Make chips navigational.** IMDb interests, Letterboxd themes, RYM descriptors and AllMusic moods all link to browse lists. GoodWatch already has mood pages and Discover pillar filters, so linking tags gives the space a second use.
9. **Hide spoilers and sensitive items behind a count.** AniList's "Show n spoiler tags" and StoryGraph's "see all" warnings.
10. **Radar charts, pies and gauges do not appear in the strongest examples**, and the perception research advises against them (§2). Flavour "spirals" and wheels are reference or brand art, not tools for reading values.

### Open questions to settle before a prototype

- **Distinctiveness rule for "top traits".** Raw scores favour attributes that are high for every title (direction, acting). A z-score against the genre or catalogue (D4, D9) needs precomputed stats. Does the existing `highlightKeys` already do this well enough?
- **Trust in the boolean flags.** A wrong "Good for kids" (D6) or a missing violence warning (D8) costs more than a wrong mood tag. Show them as softer words ("Mostly fine for teens"), or only show positives?
- **`essenceText` and `content_advisories`** are dropped from the details payload today. D2 and D8 would benefit from bringing them back.
- **Prototype names.** If any direction is prototyped, keep the labels user-facing ("traits", "for date night"), not internal ones (pillar or DNA).
