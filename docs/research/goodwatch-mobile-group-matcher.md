# First GoodWatch mobile product: choose something together tonight

Research and source access date: **2026-10-20**. Status: **research; recommendations and proposed targets, not an approved architecture or product decision**.

## Recommendation and useful dissent

**Build toward a focused iPhone/Android matcher for couples and small groups sharing one screen, with an account-free browser join path. Validate the choosing journey before substantial native work. Provisionally prefer React Native with Expo for the native clients, reusing GoodWatch's server capabilities through a deliberately bounded API.** Keep the later simplified GoodWatch companion as a separate product brief; it can share identity, catalog, availability and pure TypeScript rules without becoming a second implementation of the backend or forcing the same UI everywhere.

The product promise to test is **“Everyone gets a say; we help you finish choosing something you can actually watch tonight.”** Swiping is the interaction, not the differentiation. Use a small common deck, private votes, a shortlist and an explicit final confirmation. Do not mistake the first overlapping like, a provider click, or an app switch for successful viewing.

Useful dissent:

- **A browser product could be enough initially.** Direct competitors already advertise guest matching without installation. A link that requires several friends to install an app is an avoidable obstacle. A browser concierge pilot is the quickest way to discover whether better candidates and a bounded decision beat an ordinary chat or shortlist. This does not replace the user's intended native product; it tests its core before building it.
- **A good shortlist may outperform a long swipe deck.** Compare both in usability sessions. “Tinder for movies” is a useful explanation, but an infinite stream could reproduce the problem of endless browsing.
- **Unanimity protects the least enthusiastic person but gets harder with larger groups.** Keep it as the default acceptance rule; allow an explicit compromise round, not an undisclosed majority rule.
- **Reuse is substantial but incomplete.** The checkout contains discovery, member/guest preferences and evidence-based watchability. It does not establish native authentication, group consensus, secure guest room membership, live rooms or reliable provider app handoff. The evidence and gaps are below.

Suggested first scope: **movies; two to six people; one physical viewing location; one country; subscription/free/ad-supported offers; optional time and Mood**. Test three-to-six-person rooms alongside couples, but optimize the first use for two. Remote groups and starting a show are credible follow-ons with materially different availability and progress questions.

## Narrow MVP journey to test

Everything in this section is a **recommendation**, including limits and timings.

1. **Create tonight's room.** Host taps “Choose together”, uses a nickname and creates a room without mandatory registration. Confirm the viewing country and services available **on the screen being used tonight**. Offer a maximum movie length and an optional Mood; sensible defaults permit skipping both. Offer “More options” for language/content exclusions without making every person complete a questionnaire. Location is a manual country choice, not GPS permission.
2. **Invite and gather.** Share one HTTPS invitation, show its QR and a short manual code. The link opens the installed app or the same room in a phone browser. The guest enters a nickname, sees who is present and the shared constraints, and marks ready. Optional sign-in may import private Seen/Not interested/preferences; it must not interrupt participation. The host reviews the roster and starts with everyone ready. A participant can object to a constraint before starting.
3. **Present a bounded common deck.** Start with about 20 movies, with another small round available if useful. These are experimental numbers. Everyone gets the same stable candidate set/order and proceeds independently. Each card shows title/year, runtime, a short description, useful Mood/content information and service evidence. Require no prior taste quiz. Mix broadly appealing choices with less obvious eligible titles; avoid the same franchise occupying much of the deck.
4. **Vote privately.** Visible buttons “Would watch tonight” and “Pass tonight” do the same thing as swipes. Include undo, a “Seen” reason and optional “Rewatch is okay”; do not convert a pass to enduring Not interested, a like to Want to See, or a session vote to a rating. Exclude a title from this room if any participant says Seen unless that participant permits rewatching it. Persistent member marks retain their existing meanings. Include a separate content/availability veto when needed; a veto survives into any compromise round.
5. **Find a small acceptable shortlist.** A title all required participants like can be offered as an early result, with “Choose this” or “See a few more”; do not close the session automatically. Otherwise finish the common round, put up to three unanimously acceptable candidates on the shortlist, and ask each person for their preferred one. Equal-weight preferences break ties; an agreed seeded random choice can resolve an exact tie. Everyone still confirms they would watch the winner. “I would watch that” and “we picked that” are separate events.
6. **Choose and hand off.** Re-evaluate the finalists' watchability for the shared-screen context. Show included/free-with-ads labels and the evidence age, allow a provider choice, then offer a truthful destination label. Use a tested title URL where available; otherwise “Find on TMDB”/“Open service and search for this title”, not “Play now”. Keep title/year and a copy action visible on returning. This MVP does not promise to launch the living-room TV, select a streaming profile or start playback from a phone.
7. **Finish clearly.** Persist one final room choice and show it to everyone. Allow “Change choice” to reopen deliberation explicitly; do not let a stale vote change a completed room. Ask an optional “Did you start watching?” later in the same session/return visit, without requiring notifications. Track choice, handoff and self-reported start separately. Offer optional account creation and an explicit Want to See action only after success; no forced account wall or paywall in the shared first session is recommended for validation.

### Edge cases that belong in the first design

| Situation | Proposed behavior and rationale |
|---|---|
| Couple versus three-to-six-person group | Couples can accept an early mutual like quickly. Larger groups should see round progress and a bounded shortlist so one slow device does not create an endless race. Start with the same acceptance rule for both; measure failure separately by group size. |
| No common likes | Explain “No choice everyone accepted yet”. Offer another small deck or a group-approved relaxation of **one soft constraint** such as Mood/runtime. Never silently drop a country, provider or content restriction. If the group opts into compromise, collect private “Okay tonight”/veto responses on a few promising titles; label the result as an agreed compromise, never unanimous enthusiasm. If someone still vetoes every option, finish as no choice rather than inventing agreement. |
| No eligible titles before voting | Distinguish no verified offers from unknown/stale availability. Show how many candidates a changed service/Mood/time setting could recover, if reliably computable. “Browse without verified availability” can be an explicit exploratory mode, but must lose the watch-now guarantee. |
| Late join | Freeze required membership when a round starts. A late arrival waits for the next round or the group agrees to reopen the round, reevaluate eligibility and require their votes on the finalists. A result must name the membership version it was accepted under. Do not make a newcomer silently invalidate an earlier choice. |
| Disconnection or app backgrounding | Keep membership and accepted votes. Presence is informative; a socket closing is not consent to leave. Reconnect restores acknowledged state. Host can propose removing an absent person with a visible roster change; restart/finalize against the revised roster explicitly. A couple cannot accidentally turn into a solo “match”. |
| Host leaves | Keep a short reconnect window, then let a present participant take over via a server-controlled transition. Avoid making ordinary voting depend on the host's phone staying awake. |
| Duplicate/Seen titles | Canonicalize media-type plus catalog ID, apply established duplicate aliases, and deduplicate the room deck. Do not merge remakes or sequels by name. Keep a room-level presented/Seen set across retries. Existing Seen data helps only for people who deliberately connect it. |
| Mis-swipe or opinion change | Undo/latest vote is allowed during the round. Revisions invalidate affected provisional shortlist counts; committed choice changes require reopening. “Pass tonight” remains distinct from catalog Skip, which currently means passed in the taste quiz. |
| Accessibility | Entire flow works through labelled buttons, screen reader and keyboard/switch access; no gesture, colour or haptic is the sole signal. Support larger text, reduced motion, adequate focus/contrast and readable progress. Offer a compact list/card alternative. Test these on real devices; native framework support alone is insufficient. |
| Vote privacy | Default to only aggregate readiness/progress and shared acceptable titles. Do not transmit other participants' individual choices to clients, including the host. Final unanimous acceptance necessarily reveals that everyone accepted that finalist; in small rooms some preferences can still be inferred. Explain this limit instead of promising anonymity. No raw-vote public activity feed or session replay capture of votes. |

### Exclude initially

Exclude synchronized playback, streaming-account credential connections, background watch detection, TV casting/remote control, chat/social discovery, permanent friend graphs, full tracking/import/statistics UI, offline room operation, elaborate gamification, paid/rental comparisons and per-room LLM calls. Each adds integration or product overhead without proving a faster shared choice. Explicitly exclude automatic rating/watch-history writes: they would conflate choice with viewing and conflict with GoodWatch's [watch semantics, ADR 0008 lines 30–51](../adr/0008-one-row-per-watch.md#L30-L51) and [member-driven show states, ADR 0009 lines 5–26](../adr/0009-tracking-is-a-state-machine.md#L5-L26).

Show selection is deferred, not ruled out: “start a show” versus “continue a shared show” needs episode identity, progress and episode availability. Remote groups are deferred because each screen needs its own country/service eligibility and, if desired, separate synchronization software.

### Attribution, licensing and launch limits

**Verified external constraints:** TMDB's [official FAQ](https://developer.themoviedb.org/docs/faq) says its API is free for noncommercial use with attribution; revenue-oriented use requires commercial licensing discussions. It requires its approved logo and notice in an About/Credits section. Its [movie watch-provider documentation](https://developer.themoviedb.org/reference/movie-watch-providers) and [show equivalent](https://developer.themoviedb.org/reference/tv-series-watch-providers) require JustWatch attribution and explicitly say these responses do **not** return full deep links; the returned TMDB URL leads to viewing options. JustWatch's [partner API documentation](https://apis.justwatch.com/docs/api/) has its own branded-link/attribution requirements. That is a possible licensed integration path, not evidence GoodWatch has access.

**Unknown:** Existing GoodWatch agreements, whether they cover new mobile products/territories, commercial use, image redistribution/caching, scraped provider links, provider marks and the other sources underlying the GoodWatch score. Repository code demonstrates collection/use, not permission. Obtain an owner review of existing terms/agreements before a public release or a monetization decision; do not infer either permission or a violation from the presence of a crawler. No provider access or universal streaming entitlement API was established by this research.

**Recommendation:** Keep upstream API credentials on the server. Ship attribution and an honest link fallback as part of the product. Test real provider destinations with actual installed apps, account/profile states and target countries. An HTTP URL that opens somewhere is insufficient evidence that the correct title is available under the viewer's plan or starts playing. Do not use the code comment that many offers lack direct URLs as a measured coverage statistic; coverage was not sampled here.

### Invitations, guest fallback and accounts

Use a stable HTTPS room URL on a GoodWatch-controlled domain. Configure Apple associated domains/AASA and Android verified App Links; [Expo's linking overview](https://docs.expo.dev/linking/overview/) documents installed-app routing and website fallback, and recommends development builds for link testing. [Android's platform guidance](https://developer.android.com/training/app-links) describes verified web links. A custom scheme alone is not an adequate invitation: it gives an uninstalled guest no useful destination.

The browser destination should let guests join immediately, with an optional install/open-app affordance. Keep room URLs and one-time membership exchanges distinct. After an app-store detour, do not assume the original invite is automatically restored: preserve the code visibly and test a return-to-link/code path. Test cold/warm links, in-app messaging browsers, QR scanning, expired/full rooms and a browser participant switching into the app. Linking authentication must not create a duplicate participant or expose their votes.

[Better Auth's first-party Expo integration](https://github.com/better-auth/better-auth/blob/main/docs/content/docs/integrations/expo.mdx) documents server/client plugins, secure storage, callback schemes and cookie-prefix configuration. That supports a possible integration, **not compatibility proof with GoodWatch's custom adapter and guards**. Native auth should be a bounded spike using the existing account service. No mandatory OAuth or new identity provider is needed just to test guest rooms.

### Product separation, distribution and costs

“At least two apps” establishes two intended product experiences; it does **not** by itself settle whether there should be two binaries/store listings, one app with distinct entry points, or a native matcher plus a later web experience. Keep the matcher independently understandable. Decide store packaging later from user overlap, branding, release cadence and whether the companion offers a meaningfully different job. Two separate apps may share packages/backend/accounts while preserving separate navigation. Apple [guidelines 4.2 and 4.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) require adequate functionality and address multiple Bundle IDs for the same app; they do not ban distinct products sharing a backend.

Current base distribution fees: [Apple Developer Program](https://developer.apple.com/programs) lists **US$99/year**; [Google Play Console](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en) lists **US$25 once**, with local pricing/taxes and enrollment details to confirm. New personal Google accounts created after November 13, 2023 face a [closed-test requirement of 12 opted-in testers for 14 continuous days](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en); account type matters. Use TestFlight and Play testing tracks before release, not “installable on my phone” as distribution readiness.

[Expo pricing](https://expo.dev/pricing) currently offers a limited free build tier and Starter at **US$19/month plus usage**, including US$45 build credit. EAS is optional: [local builds](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/local-app-overview/) are supported with the platform toolchains. Neither framework removes device access, release engineering or ongoing maintenance. Budget also for room connections/polling, durable state, observability, poster/CDN traffic, catalog/licensing and support. No traffic-volume or monthly infrastructure total can be responsibly estimated from this research.

Guest-first reduces initial auth work but does not remove privacy/disclosure obligations. If account creation ships, [Apple's account-deletion guidance](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/) requires initiating deletion in the app. Inspect the actual existing deletion journey before native release; this report did not verify one. Review current social-login/store requirements if Google/Apple sign-in is included. No paid tier is proposed by this research.

**What could change the recommendation:** A working DOM/Capacitor prototype that satisfies device/accessibility/link tests at materially lower maintenance cost; strong Flutter team experience; Expo/auth/package conflicts that cannot be isolated cleanly; or evidence that installation is an unacceptable barrier and browser repeat use is sufficient. Make that decision after the spikes, not from dependency names.

## Conceptual session/backend design

This is a **design hypothesis**, intentionally short of a schema or technology selection.

### Membership, viewing context and candidate deck

A room owns the constraints, host capability, current round/membership version, ordered title IDs, private per-member votes and chosen result. The server issues a separate unguessable guest credential per participant; a nickname and room code are never authentication. An account can attach to a participant deliberately. Invites are revocable/expiring join capabilities; short codes are rate-limited discovery handles, not sufficient credentials for reading existing private votes. Require authorization on room reads, vote writes, live subscriptions, roster changes and finalization.

For one shared screen, candidate eligibility is **a title available through at least one service usable on that screen in its country**. Do not intersect every friend's home subscriptions: a guest with no subscription can still watch beside the host. Equally, do not blindly union all friends' services and imply all their logins are on that TV. Ask which services are usable tonight; GoodWatch cannot verify account-sharing permissions or actual entitlement from catalog metadata.

For a future remote room, candidate titles must be watchable **for every viewing screen**, each evaluated against its own country and usable offers. The title intersection matters, not merely the intersection of provider names: one person could use Netflix and another Prime if synchronized playback is not required. If a chosen external watch-party tool requires a common service, that is an additional explicit constraint. Treat the shared-screen and remote contexts as different policies, not a toggle that unions provider IDs.

Generate the bounded deck once on the server from presentable canonical titles, hard eligibility/content constraints and private exclusions. Record its policy version and snapshot/order so clients do not reshuffle on reconnect. Unknown availability remains unknown; optionally verify more evidence before including a title in a strict watch-now deck. Use batches and cached catalog facts, not one external API/LLM request per swipe. Preserve already-presented/vetoed exclusions on another round; changing constraints produces a new round version.

### Matching and fairness beyond the first overlapping like

Start with equal participant weight. A vote means willingness **tonight**, not predicted taste. With three people, likes from two and an uncast third vote are pending, not a match. With binary votes, all unanimously liked titles have the same approval count: calling one “strongest” needs another signal, such as the shortlist preference round.

Candidate ranking may use a **balance of the least well-served person's fit and average fit**, plus diversity, as a later comparison against simple constrained popular/quality ordering. That protects a minority more than average-only ranking but can favor safe compromise over anyone's favorite. A strict minimum can also overreact to a noisy or missing taste profile. Use calibrated per-person relative ranks only where taste exists; a guest without taste is unknown, not zero. Do not let signed-in people get more voting weight or let a host's catalog position decide the winner.

The first MVP should therefore use transparent mechanics: common deck, all-like shortlist, optional preference ranking, explicit tie-break and final consent. Prototype “take the first mutual like” as a competing simpler policy. In larger groups, if no unanimous shortlist exists, ask permission for an “acceptable tonight” round on a few promising non-vetoed titles. Everyone must accept the final compromise; a majority vote may nominate a candidate but cannot relabel a dissenter's pass as consent. A content veto is never automatically relaxed. A no-choice outcome is legitimate.

### Live updates, correctness and durability

HTTP commands plus foreground polling can validate a tiny pilot; server-sent events or WebSockets are plausible for live roster/progress/result updates. Prefer the simplest transport that meets measured latency/reconnect needs. All are delivery mechanisms, not a second authority for votes. Mobile background suspension requires resuming from a server snapshot/version, not assuming a permanent connection.

Votes must be idempotent by room/round/member/title and operation ID/revision. A retry returns the accepted result; it does not add another like. A newer undo/change must not be overwritten by a delayed older request. The server validates title membership, current round, participant capability and whether the room is open. Mark unacknowledged local votes pending; retry only while valid. Finalization atomically checks the required membership version and votes, then commits one result. Concurrent finalizers, reconnect replay and a roster change must never produce competing winners. Do not automatically remove disconnected people from the quorum.

**Important repository constraint:** [ADR 0003](../adr/0003-redis-cluster-of-masters-with-volatile-lfu.md) deliberately permits eviction and partial slot failures because cache data is rebuildable; [ADR 0004](../adr/0004-valkey-8-from-the-official-image.md) carries that topology into Valkey. A new room's sole copy of votes cannot inherit those assumptions silently. Crate share-list code also calls out delayed read visibility. Evaluate atomic updates/read-after-write, crash recovery and expiry semantics in a spike; no choice of PostgreSQL, Crate, dedicated Valkey or a managed realtime service is approved here. A process-local room map alone would lose acknowledged votes on restart and diverge across instances.

### Guest security, retention and minimal analytics

Proposed lifecycle: lobby → active round → shortlist → chosen/no-choice → expired. Suggest a **24-hour room lifetime** for the pilot, with explicit expiry shown and a short window to return to the result. Pick raw-vote/diagnostic retention deliberately and delete it on schedule; do not silently turn temporary swipes into permanent personal profiles. These are proposed durations, not existing policy. A disconnected member retains membership until an explicit change/expiry.

Use TLS, secure native credential storage and secure web cookies where applicable; keep admin and participant capabilities separate. Join exchanges must resist code guessing and repeated identity creation, limit capacity/rate, allow invite rotation and avoid leaking full roster/votes on invalid codes. Protect browser mutation endpoints against cross-site requests; a native app needs a real credential contract, not a secret compiled into the binary. Keep invitations/guest credentials out of analytics, error URLs, link previews and referrers; design preview endpoints so a messaging crawler cannot consume a single-use join or expose the room. Server validation must not trust client-provided totals or a claimed member ID.

Measure a small funnel: room created, guest joined/ready, round started, shortlist presented, choice confirmed, handoff attempted, optional start-watching confirmation, no-choice/expired. Record coarse group size, guest/native/browser mode, time-to-choice, deck count and failure reason; separate invite waiting time from active choosing time. Use pseudonymous room/member IDs with a stated retention period. Avoid raw private likes/passes, room tokens/nicknames and captured swipe screens in analytics. Measure technical latency and retry/reconnect failures separately. Current telemetry files are being edited in the checkout; their presence is not proof these events or consent rules are already implemented.

## Validate before substantial build

### Small concierge/usability study

**Proposed study, not research already performed:** Recruit about eight couples and four groups of three-to-six adults, including iPhone/Android mixes and at least one browser-only participant per group where practical. The sample is for finding friction, not statistical market validation. Use actual services in the intended launch country. Include a screen-reader/larger-text/reduced-motion usability pass with people who use those features where possible.

First observe how each group currently chooses and whether they end up watching. Then run a clickable/mobile-browser concierge flow where the researcher prepares a short, currently verified eligible deck. Compare a swipe round plus shortlist against a three-to-five-title shortlist and the first-mutual-like policy, counterbalancing order across groups. Do not advertise an algorithm when candidates were hand selected. Follow with two unmoderated evenings for willing groups to distinguish novelty from repeat usefulness; no contacts/accounts were created for this report.

| Hypothesis | What to measure | Proposed directional target / decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Guests will participate without derailing the evening | Link opened → ready; requests for help; install/signup abandonment | At least 90% of invited test participants join without moderator help in under 45 seconds. Report small-sample counts and failures, not just a percentage. |
| The product finishes choosing faster | Host create → choice, last required ready → choice, baseline comparison | Median create-to-choice ≤3 minutes for couples and ≤5 minutes for larger groups; active choosing ≤2/3 minutes respectively. These are product targets, not competitor performance evidence. |
| It produces an accepted choice | All required members confirm; eligible sessions with choice within five minutes | Aim for ≥80% choice completion in eligible sessions. Count no-choice/abandonment and strict-filter failures separately; do not remove them to improve a headline rate. |
| Availability/handoff earns trust | Independently check finalists; observe actual provider destination; ask whether watching started | Aim for no wrong-country/paid-as-included claims in the small pilot; log unknowns. Aim for ≥70% of sessions with a confirmed choice to self-report starting it that evening. Keep clicks separate from starts; this sample cannot prove catalog-wide accuracy. |
| People feel equally represented | Each person's willingness, fairness comments, veto/no-match behavior | No participant reports the app declared a choice they had rejected. Compare group-size failures and who consistently compromises. |
| It has value beyond novelty | Voluntary reuse on a second real occasion; reasons not to return | Seek evidence of independent reuse and preference over the group's prior method. Do not call this a retention estimate or use prompted repeat sessions as organic retention. |

Interpret misses before expanding scope: bad candidates, too much setup, inability to finish, trust failures and invitation friction require different fixes. Native polish cannot rescue an unhelpful deck. If the shortlist consistently wins, keep playful swipes optional rather than preserving them for the metaphor.

### Bounded technical spikes and main risks

1. **Invite/identity/auth:** Two real platforms plus mobile web; installed/uninstalled cold/warm links, QR/code, background/return, expired invites, browser-to-native handoff and no duplicate member. Exercise existing-account sign-in/revocation against the actual auth flags/custom adapter. Verify a clean package boundary despite differing React versions.
2. **Availability and licensing:** In the selected country/services, sample approximately 100 candidates and 20 finalists across recent/older movies, missing data, ad tiers and add-on channels. Compare evidence age, unknown/no-match and real provider offers. Inspect existing agreements/attribution scope with the owner; verify actual title destinations on iPhone/Android. This is a proposed spike, not a performed catalog audit.
3. **Room correctness:** Six simulated clients plus real phones: duplicated/delayed votes, simultaneous undo/finalize, disconnect, process restart, two server instances, expired guest identity, late join and host loss. Require one winner for one membership version, acknowledged-vote survival under the promised recovery policy and no cross-member vote disclosure. Select storage/live transport only after this.
4. **Deck quality/cost:** Compare constrained popularity/quality plus diversity against optional balanced member taste. Measure cold and warm candidate time, per-room source calls, availability coverage and search independence. Proposed target: initial eligible deck in under two seconds warm and foreground vote/result propagation under one second on normal test networks; publish failure distributions, not only the median.

Main risks are crowded positioning, scarce acceptable intersection for larger groups, wrong or stale offers, privacy leakage by client payloads/analytics, install/link friction, unresolved data rights, and assuming web auth APIs are native-ready. Each has a validation action above. No current evidence justifies a market forecast, guaranteed time-to-watch, full provider launch promise or a large new infrastructure stack.

### Phased next steps and decisions for the user

**Phase 0:** Choose a launch segment/country and run the concierge comparison. **Phase 1:** If the choosing outcome is promising, run the bounded link/auth, availability and room-correctness spikes; make the framework/storage decisions with those results. **Phase 2:** Build a small private iOS/Android pilot with browser joining and explicit completion metrics. **Phase 3:** Improve repeat use and then scope the simplified companion independently, sharing established services and rules. No implementation phase is authorized by this note.

The few decisions that materially change direction:

1. **Initial audience and setting:** Couples sharing one screen first, or remote/mixed-country groups from day one? Which launch country, language and a few services matter most? Remote-first changes eligibility and handoff enough to change the MVP.
2. **Meaning of “match”:** Is the goal a quick mutually acceptable movie, or the best shared choice after a short round? Is a clearly labelled compromise round acceptable, with each person retaining a veto? The report recommends a quick choice with optional shortlist, but usability should test the simpler alternative.
3. **Native/distribution and commercial intent:** Are public store presence and two distinct branded products immediate requirements, or can an invited browser pilot validate first and companion packaging stay open? Is early monetization intended? Commercial intent affects data-license review; packaging affects release/auth/link work.

Until answered, the working assumptions are shared-screen couples/small groups, movies first, a browser validation step, native Expo as a candidate, and no final decision about separate companion store listings. No country or monetization plan is inferred from the repository owner’s location.
