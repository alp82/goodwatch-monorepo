Researched **2026-11-16**. Research only: no filings, contact, access requests, app changes or local app server. The separate `sitewide-indexing.md` investigation was not edited. No personal contact details belong in this document. The owner-adaptable counter-notice draft is outside the repository at `~/goodwatch-private/copyright/counter-notice-draft.md`.

## Findings and limits

**Google publicly reports 11 copyright requests naming goodwatch.app, covering 28 URL submissions: 26 removed and 2 not in the index.** This is substantially more history than the two emails in the brief. It is **not evidence of 28 distinct pages**: a URL can occur in multiple requests. Full paths were unavailable, so deduplication and the sitemap intersection for all submissions remain unknown. [Google domain report](https://transparencyreport.google.com/copyright/domains/goodwatch.app)

**The owner-reported June 19, 2026 claim remains unreconciled.** Its five supplied URLs were checked, but neither its date nor supplied Lumen ID matches the 11 entries below. Do not silently add a twelfth notice or add five to 28: the June communication might describe another request, repeat older removals, or contain a reference/date error. Two emails do not establish two separate complaints. Google identifies Remove Your Media LLC as a reporting organization, representing underlying owners, rather than necessarily the copyright owner itself. [Request inventory](https://transparencyreport.google.com/transparencyreport/api/v3/copyright/requests/summary?domain=goodwatch.app)

**The five current pages look like ordinary discovery/reference pages, not full-film or episode delivery.** That supports a mistake/misidentification challenge to the alleged full-video infringement. It does not establish that every image, synopsis or embedded upload is authorized, or determine the merits of the unseen historical complaints.

**Search effect, in two lines:** Individual removals are confirmed; requests on December 14 and 16, 2025 make copyright action a plausible contributor around the reported December 16 indexing loss.<br>
Google documents domain ranking demotion for significant valid-removal volume, with no numerical cutoff; 11 requests/28 submissions do not prove that it applied, stopped crawling, or caused the loss of approximately 1,100 indexed pages. [Google ranking systems](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide#removal-based-demotion-systems)

Each successful page has metadata, synopsis, ratings, cast information, images from `image.tmdb.org`, and external streaming-provider choices. No full movie/episode file or player for one was identified. This is a current page/code audit, not proof of their contents on every historical notice date.

The image builder points to TMDB's CDN in [tmdb-image.ts](../../../goodwatch-webapp/app/utils/tmdb-image.ts); the importer copies TMDB `overview` into GoodWatch `synopsis` in [movies_and_shows.py](../../../goodwatch-flows/windmill/f/main_db/sync/movies_and_shows.py). ### Images, prose and permissions

TMDB permits API use subject to its license and attribution but expressly does **not own all images/data** supplied. A TMDB key or CDN URL cannot establish clearance from the underlying studio or prose author. A synopsis or poster can raise a copyright issue independently of any full-video allegation. Neither the owner's actual agreements nor historical asset permissions were available for verification. A categorical “nothing infringes” conclusion would exceed the evidence. [TMDB API FAQ](https://developer.themoviedb.org/docs/faq)

The more detailed [TMDB API terms](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use), sections 1–3, also restrict AI/ML uses under the standard grant, classify destination/search/interactive query-response uses as requiring a separate written agreement, and impose a six-month caching restriction. Check the actual agreement and applicability to GoodWatch's AI/search features. These are separate contractual questions, not proof that the complaining agents own these assets. Existing [licensing research](backlinks/04-listings-registries.md) already flags them.

The reported Coraline/anime mismatch supports poor identification, but a templated mistake does not automatically invalidate a properly identified image claim or establish intentional abuse. We could not read the full historical claims or test their alleged material against archived versions. **Assessment: plausible false positives for full-film/episode allegations; unresolved legality for specific assets and unseen complaints.**

### Improvements recommended regardless of counter notices

- **Public attribution:** The footer contains TMDB's logo, but the exact required non-endorsement notice was absent from all five successful title HTML responses; in app source it appears in the TMDB import-connect panel. Provide a prominent public About/Credits attribution with the approved logo and: “This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.” [TMDB attribution requirements](https://developer.themoviedb.org/docs/faq)
- **Rights records:** Confirm the actual TMDB agreement, permissible AI/search use, cache practices, and a defensible rights basis for images and copied prose. Clear, replace or remove disputed assets where that basis is missing. Attribution alone is not clearance. Do not claim comprehensive TMDB licensing in a counter notice without evidence.
- **Video provenance:** Retain channel/source/official fields and select authorized, embed-enabled uploads; audit clips and featurettes as well as trailers. Respect removals and restrictions; continue using the ordinary player. No changes were made here.
- **Accurate legal description:** The existing disclaimer refers to `goodwatch.com`; correct the domain in a later authorized code task. Explain the discovery/third-party-player model accurately. A disclaimer does not cure infringement. [Disclaimer source](../../../goodwatch-webapp/app/routes/disclaimer.tsx)

## Ranking, indexing and the December 2025 timing

Google's current policy says a **significant volume of valid copyright removal requests** can be used to demote other content on the same site. It publishes no numeric trigger, so neither “five is harmless” nor “hundreds are required” is supported. Hundreds of valid requests would provide stronger evidence of volume than a handful; that is an inference, not a published boundary. There is no documented equivalence between notice count, URL count, and the signal's weight. [Ranking-system guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide#removal-based-demotion-systems)

Here the history matters: four submissions on December 14 and four on December 16 predate or coincide with the reported loss. Four more on December 17, one on December 22 and nine in January follow it. Later requests cannot explain the initial drop. Even before considering duplicate paths, eight December 14/16 submissions cannot directly remove approximately 1,100 different pages. A separate domain signal is a possible hypothesis, not an observed fact.

Legal delisting, ranking demotion, crawl frequency and index inclusion are different outcomes. This investigation establishes the first, not the other three. Do not stop the independent technical/quality/indexing investigation. If the June date is accurate, that later notice alone cannot explain a December 2025 event. No official source located promises that successful counter notices reset the site signal, restore previous rankings, or restore crawl frequency on a timetable.

## Recommended response and what it requires

**Prepare targeted counter notices for genuinely mistaken removals, after obtaining the complete complaints and confirming the facts the owner will attest to. Do not mass-file all 11 based solely on the five current pages.** Prioritize identifying the December 14/16 cases because of their timing; check their exact URLs and allegations first. If claims concern unlicensed images or prose, resolve those rights and get copyright advice before making a sworn noninfringement claim. The supplied draft is preparation only.

Use Google's [official Web Search counter-notice route](https://support.google.com/legal/contact/lr_counternotice?product=websearch), which redirects to the [current landing form](https://reportcontent.google.com/landing/counter_notice?product=websearch&hl=en). Browser verification showed the initial **Reference ID** field, explicitly referring to the restriction message sent via Search Console. The direct form requires Google sign-in or email verification. Later authenticated fields were not viewed; no reference or contact details were entered. Use Google's case/reference ID, not a substituted Lumen notice number. [Google legal instructions](https://support.google.com/legal/answer/1120734?hl=en)

The owner must supply privately:

- **Legal identity and authority:** Legal name, entity/representative authority where applicable, full mailing address and country, telephone, contact email, dated physical/electronic signature.
- **Case and evidence:** Google Reference ID(s), exact removed URLs, full complaints, identified works/assets, historical/current screenshots or captures, and actual permissions/agreements or another defensible legal basis. Handle each case as the form instructs; the same path may need addressing under multiple notices.
- **Sworn good faith:** A statement under penalty of perjury that removal resulted from mistake or misidentification.
- **Jurisdiction and service:** Consent to the federal district court for the address's district if in the US; if outside the US, consent to any US judicial district in which the provider may be found. Agree to accept service from the notifying claimant or its agent. The private draft leaves the applicable choice for the owner.

These requirements derive from [17 USC 512(g)(3)](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html#512), with procedure summarized by the [Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/512/). The counter notice is furnished to the original complainant: **the name, address and other submitted details are not confidential from that party**. Google may also forward notices to Lumen with some personal information removed; do not rely on anonymity. Knowing material misrepresentation can create damages/costs/fees liability under section 512(f), and the claimant can bring court action. This is a legal declaration with litigation exposure, not a routine SEO request.

**Expected result:** Google may reinstate the specific links after reviewing a valid counter notice. The statutory process describes restoration after 10–14 business days absent notice of a qualifying court action; this is not a guaranteed Search indexing/ranking deadline. Reinstatement removes a legal obstacle for those links; normal indexing/ranking decisions remain. Preserve all receipt, forwarding and outcome messages privately and inspect the exact URLs after a decision. [Google counter-notice policy](https://support.google.com/legal/answer/1120734?hl=en), [statutory restoration process](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html#512)
