# GoodWatch's sitewide indexing problem

## Answer

**Most likely: Google assigns low crawl demand and insufficient differentiated value to GoodWatch's inner catalog, potentially reinforced by limited independent recognition. Confidence: moderate about that cause, high that discovery/submission alone has not solved it.** The December 16, 2025 collapse falls inside the confirmed December 11–29 core update; that is a plausible reassessment trigger, not proof of a penalty. No announced December 2025 spam update or new scaled-content/site-reputation policy matches the date. [Google's update incident](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/DsirqJ1gpPRgVQeccPRv), [ranking history](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history).

**Recommend one bounded value experiment: improve five existing title pages, leave five matched pages unchanged, and observe for six weeks, with an eight-week hard stop.** Continue only if at least three improved pages stay indexed in two consecutive weekly readings, outperform controls by at least two pages, and receive nonbranded impressions. A content verdict requires fresh successful crawls; no recrawls means the value hypothesis was untested, not disproved. A 100-page indexability restriction is a documented alternative below, not a proven cure or an automatic second round.

**Do not launch during the current core rollout.** Google's November 2026 core update began November 10 and is still open on the dashboard checked November 16. Prepare the pilot, establish a baseline after completion plus one full week, then start its clock. Recovery during the rollout must not be credited to the pilot. [Active incident](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/WLxcJyC6jMWMfryxsKMc), [Google's analysis timing](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates).

Research date: **2026-11-16**. Source snapshot: fetched `origin/main` at `b4d1308d6`. Research only; nothing below authorizes implementation or changes the existing owner decisions.

## 2. How strong is the site-quality hypothesis?

### What the status actually means

Google's [Page indexing help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7440203?hl=en) defines “Discovered - currently not indexed” as a known URL that has not been crawled, typically rescheduled because crawling was expected to overload the site. It is **not a diagnostic label meaning “low quality”**. “Crawled - currently not indexed” means a fetch happened without inclusion; it also does not name a specific quality defect.

The stronger inference comes from the combined evidence: prolonged exclusion, successful sampled fetches, downloaded sitemaps, working links, some fetched canonical pages declined, and little retained progress. Google separates crawl capacity from demand; demand depends on quality, relevance, popularity and perceived inventory. A healthy host can have low demand. [Crawl guidance](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget).

There is unusually relevant primary guidance: in [July 2023 office hours](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/july), Google answered whether consolidating eight million used-product URLs into two million would fix this exact status. It said the outcome could not be predicted and that overall website quality usually matters more than just capacity. Reducing a count without improving the site might accomplish nothing. This supports investigating quality; it does not establish GoodWatch's diagnosis.

### Ranked signals and the relevant Google guidance

1. **Insufficient clearly verifiable incremental value across a large catalog — strongest content hypothesis, moderate confidence.** The shared synopsis, credits, ratings and offers do useful product work but compete with authoritative source catalogs. The category samples add little explanation of individual selections. Google's [helpful-content questions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) ask for original analysis and substantial value beyond sources and competing results. They do not prescribe a word count or unique-text percentage. The fingerprint, career comparisons, streaming history and personal tools are counterevidence to “nothing original.”
2. **Scaled inferred analysis with weak visible provenance — material quality risk, not a proven spam violation.** [Generation source](../../../goodwatch-flows/windmill/f/dna/generate/fetch.py) asks an LLM for 74 scores, suitability/advisory flags, essence tags and experience prose. [Publishing source](../../../goodwatch-flows/windmill/f/sync/copy/dna_data.py) carries the analysis into catalog data. This establishes automation, not the number of live pages generated or universal absence of review. Today's [Summary component](../../../goodwatch-webapp/app/ui/details/TitleSummary.tsx) displays the catalog synopsis, while [Q&A](../../../goodwatch-webapp/app/ui/details/titleQuestions.ts) and [feeling answers](../../../goodwatch-webapp/app/ui/details/fingerprintQuestions.ts) mostly use deterministic templates over facts and inferred scores. Calling every current answer freshly LLM-written would be wrong. Google's [AI guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) calls for accuracy, manual review and useful creation context. Its [spam policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#scaled-content) targets scale used primarily to manipulate rankings without user value, regardless of AI or human authorship. Automation alone does not establish that purpose.
3. **Large eligible URL graph relative to demonstrated demand — strong existence evidence, uncertain causal weight.** Sitemap membership caps discovery suggestions, not indexability. Existing title/person links, pagination and now title subpages expose additional surfaces. Google's [faceted-navigation guidance](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/faceted-navigation) explains how combinatorial URLs can crowd out useful discovery. However, GoodWatch already disallows Discover/person query facets and suppresses server-rendered person-filter hrefs; the [person investigation](person-traffic-503.md#where-the-urls-come-from) measured hostile enumeration, not Google ignoring those bounds. Do not assign the scraper's request volume to Google.
4. **Limited independent recognition — plausible reinforcing factor, incompletely measured.** The [earlier diagnosis](tweet-indexing-diagnosis.md#referring-domains-measured-lower-bound-unknown-total) verified at least three referring domains, including the project's own repository. That is a lower bound, not zero links or a complete authority score. Google [uses links for relevance and discovery](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable). No official minimum backlink count or controlled GoodWatch link experiment exists.
5. **Thinness, duplication or spam across every page — too strong as a conclusion.** Source reuse is direct: [TMDB copy code](../../../goodwatch-flows/windmill/f/sync/copy/tmdb_details.py) maps overview to synopsis, and live offer links go through JustWatch. Repeated facts are not a general duplicate-content penalty. Google's [ranking-systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide) describes deduplication and both page and site signals, not an all-or-nothing site quality score. There is no evidence of third-party parasite publishing exploiting GoodWatch's established reputation. Member content is not automatically site reputation abuse. Current manual-action/security/removal status cannot be established from the weekly API; the earlier owner check found no manual action.

The December drop predates the October 2026 `/new` expansion. That expansion cannot explain the original loss, though current indexable scope can affect a recovery attempt. Today's better title UI and analysis likewise cannot reconstruct what Google judged in December 2025.

## 3. What recovery evidence actually supports

| Approach | Best available evidence | What it establishes for GoodWatch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prune or noindex weak material; consolidate overlapping pages | First-hand [GSQi December 2020 case studies](https://www.gsqi.com/marketing-blog/december-2020-google-core-algorithm-update-part-two-case-studies/), published January 13, 2021: a niche publisher removed thin material and consolidated recurring posts, alongside technical/content repairs, then reported a 149% organic increase at the next core update. Another large site noindexed/removed thin sections, improved important content and reduced intrusive ads, reporting a 40% increase. | Credible practitioner observations, but anonymous sites, multiple simultaneous changes, no control, and traffic/ranking outcomes rather than a one-homepage indexing recovery. They do **not** show pruning alone caused the gains. |
| Add unique, reliable value | Google's [helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) and [core-update guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates) favor substantial improvements. Deletion is a last resort for material that cannot be salvaged. | Strong support for the direction; no promise that five rewrites overturn a site-level assessment. Rewording TMDB or appending generic FAQs is not a persuasive test. |
| Earn relevant independent links | Google's [link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable) supports discovery and relevance. | Mechanistic support, not evidence that a particular number of directory links recovers indexing. Continue the approved links-first plan; record publication and dates. No purchased/manufactured-link target. |
| Wait for reassessment/core updates | Google says changes may register in days or months; another core update can matter, but recovery between announced updates is possible. [Official recovery guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates). | Waiting can be necessary after real improvements. Waiting without an expenditure/time limit is not a channel strategy. |
| Cut the sitemap to 50–100 pages | Google's [July 2023 answer](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/july) explicitly warns that shrinking counts without improving quality may do nothing. | No credible controlled case was found showing this alone reliably reverses GoodWatch's exact pattern. Treat a restriction as an experiment with opportunity cost, not established treatment. |

These cases are analogies, not matched movie-catalog recoveries. Current Google guidance takes precedence over the older practitioner's claim that recovery generally requires the next announced core update. Neither a successful HTTP request nor one indexed URL is evidence that search can supply a useful audience.

## 4. Experiments and decision rules

### Common measurement and safeguards

The October 27 owner decision in #347 put links before page-value work and deferred the earlier five-page pilot; it also retained all 300 `/new` pages and a separate 30-person cohort. **Both proposals below require a deliberate revision of that plan before implementation.** This research implements neither and does not silently pass #354. The unchanged record supports closing the original gate as unsuccessful, then choosing at most one final bounded attempt or moving directly to #359.

- **Start date:** First baseline after the active core update ends plus seven days; freeze pilot membership and record deploy time and exact content version. If the rollout unexpectedly persists, review the plan by December 7 instead of waiting indefinitely. Record later core updates, major app/data changes, earned links and outages as confounds.
- **Preserve history:** Leave [index-sample.csv](index-sample.csv) and all existing record rows untouched. Continue the existing weekly job and traffic/sitemap records. Mark intentional noindex exclusions separately; a fall in indexed percentage from the old sample is not failure if those pages deliberately left the eligible set.
- **Add a sidecar, not a replacement:** For a future implementation, add `docs/research/discovery-search/sitewide-experiment-sample.csv` and an append-only `sitewide-experiment-record.csv`, with arm, URL, eligibility, change date/version, inspection state, last crawl, fetch state and both canonicals. Use the existing read-only `goodwatch-webapp/scripts/gsc.py`; extend sampling separately if automated. One weekly inspection per pilot URL is enough. Cap the experiment's weekly inspection run at **150**, including the existing 50, below a 200-call session budget; today's research used **zero**.
- **Measure the funnel separately:** Discovered → fresh successful crawl → correct canonical/index eligibility → indexed → retained for two Saturdays → nonbranded impressions → clicks → engaged landings. Use Search Analytics with its normal delay, and the #348/PostHog baseline queries after at least six weeks. Count “like”, “similar”, mood and equivalent German discovery queries for #359; title-name impressions alone are a weaker signal. An impression is not a visit. Query suppression means an absent reported query is not proof of zero demand.
- **Keep stable operating evidence:** Use existing Search Console/verified-bot summaries for fetch health; after the proxy move do not equate direct peer identity with Googlebot. Pause causal interpretation on an observed access incident, record it and repair only in a separately authorized task. No repeated manual indexing requests, new account configuration or production test server is needed for these proposals.

### Experiment A — five pages worth choosing over another catalog (recommended)

**Question:** Can a small amount of verifiable, title-specific public guidance improve retained indexing and discovery visibility relative to existing pages, while holding indexable scope constant?

Pre-register these five pairs, one improved page and one comparison page per pair. Assignment is fixed before outcomes; these are approximate comparisons, not equivalent titles or statistically powered groups.

| Improved | Comparison |
| --- | --- |
| `/show/108978-reacher` | `/show/2316-the-office` |
| `/show/456-the-simpsons` | `/show/1416-greys-anatomy` |
| `/movie/157336-interstellar` | `/movie/27205-inception` |
| `/movie/155-the-dark-knight` | `/movie/496243-parasite` |
| `/movie/238-the-godfather` | `/movie/863-toy-story-2` |

The four shows were successfully crawled and declined in the October 27 inspection; the six movies are discovery-demand probes, not known fresh fetched exclusions. At implementation baseline, record all ten again and stratify results by movie/show and prior fetch. If an ID/path is no longer valid, replace it **before assignment is frozen**, recording the reason. Do not switch controls after results appear.

**Change:** On each improved page, add an editor-checked viewing recommendation: who it suits, a concrete attention/tone caveat, and two or three alternatives explained with a specific similarity **and difference**. Use actual viewing/research evidence; never invent first-hand experience. Separate model-inferred traits from sourced facts, name the review method/date, link the relevant source/methodology, check current country offers, and resolve visible contradictions. Keep useful ratings and metadata. No keyword-count or word-count quota. Comparison pages keep their existing content; health fixes apply equally and are logged.

**Proposed code/files:** Add `goodwatch-webapp/app/content/title-editorial.json` keyed by media type and ID, plus a small SSR `app/ui/details/TitleEditorial.tsx`; mount it in `app/ui/details/TitlePage.tsx` near Summary with the existing media ID. Use `app/ui/details/TitleSummary.tsx`, `titleQuestions.ts` and `fingerprintQuestions.ts` for necessary consistency corrections on the selected pages; avoid global text changes that contaminate controls. Add clear analysis methodology to `app/routes/how-it-works.tsx` / `app/ui/how-it-works/features.ts` only if scoped or explicitly logged as a shared intervention. No sitemap/robots/canonical change. Focused editorial-render checks and the repository's landing-surface budget check belong to implementation, not this research.

**Risks:** Low search opportunity cost, but material editor time; a truthful reviewed recommendation takes effort. Member ratings, Want to See, tracking, privacy and personalization must retain their behavior. `/new`'s 300 pages and actor/person eligibility stay as they are. Shared title-component changes could affect members, payload size or all controls unless rendering is gated by ID. This tests marginal public value, not the entire site's quality; common site signals can affect both arms.

**Time box and decision:** Six weeks from deployment; allow only one extension to week eight for delayed crawls. At week six, require at least **four of five pages in each arm** to have a successful post-change crawl with the intended canonical before interpreting the comparison.

- **Continue narrowly:** At least **3/5 improved pages** are indexed on two consecutive weekly readings, at least **two more than controls**, with reported nonbranded impressions on improved pages. Expand to only five more editorial pages, not the full catalog. This is directional evidence, not causal proof with ten URLs.
- **Change course:** Both arms gain similarly or movement overlaps a core update: a sitewide change/reassessment is plausible, so retain useful improvements but do not credit editorial treatment or scale production. If fresh successful crawls occur but neither arm retains pages, the small rewrite approach failed its business test; either explicitly choose B as the final scope test or go to #359.
- **Stop active search investment:** By week eight, still no adequate fresh-crawl exposure or no retained non-home progress: record “crawl-demand test inconclusive” or “fetched but not retained,” respectively, and take #359's hold/drop decision. Positive but subthreshold results mean hold the existing pages without expansion and take the same channel decision. Keep useful app pages; stop expanding SEO work. This is a spending decision, not a claim Google can never recover.

### Experiment B — genuinely restrict the indexable catalog to 100 pages

**Alternative question:** Does a much smaller, deliberately selected public search offering gain retained coverage when the current site's general quality remains otherwise stable? Choose this instead of A if scope reduction is the agreed priority. Do not launch both together and claim to identify which caused a lift.

**Fixed cohort:** **60 titles** (include all 25 titles in the old sample, then 35 chosen by accurate offers, complete analysis and distinctive viewing usefulness), **5 people** (the five in the old sample), **10 `/new` pages**, and **25 hubs/category/product pages including `/`**. For `/new`, freeze `/new/us`, `/new/de`, and the US/DE service pairs for Netflix, Amazon Prime Video, Disney+ and Apple TV: ten total, after validating the canonical service slugs and meaningful current arrivals. Freeze the exact 100-path manifest before deployment; choose the 25 hubs/categories to keep clear routes to the retained titles and include all eight old-sample categories where useful. Record this manifest, not a rotating SQL top-100 ranking.

Use one shared, versioned proposed `goodwatch-webapp/app/content/search-cohort.json` as input to sitemap generation and route metadata. Keep self canonicals and **200/noindex, follow** for unselected catalog pages, with full member functionality. Return 404/410 only for content genuinely removed without a replacement; use a 301 only for true equivalent replacements. Do not canonicalize unrelated movies or countries into hubs. Keep noindexed pages crawlable so Google can see the instruction. [Google's noindex requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing).

**Proposed code/files:**

- **Sitemaps:** Filter `goodwatch-webapp/scripts/create_sitemaps.py` and selection in `sitemap_selection.py`, `sitemap_query.py`, `sitemap_people.py`, `sitemap_new.py` through the same manifest. Regenerate `goodwatch-webapp/public/sitemaps/` and ensure the scheduled generation path cannot restore excluded URLs; keep honest lastmod dates. This is more than editing XML once.
- **Indexability:** Add a route-scoped policy helper, proposed `app/utils/search-eligibility.ts`, and use it via `app/utils/detailsMeta.ts` for title pages, `app/routes/person.$personKey.tsx`, the two category routes `app/routes/$type.$category._index.tsx` and `$type.$category.$page.tsx`, and `app/ui/new-on-services/meta.ts` / `app/server/new-on-services-page.server.ts`. Preserve the latter's existing member/empty-page noindex behavior. Audit hubs, category pagination, awards and title subpages too: `app/ui/details/sub-page/sub-page-meta.ts` currently gives fingerprint, at-a-glance and tropes their own canonicals. Unselected catalog subpages must not remain an uncontrolled indexable escape route. Do not implement a blanket default-deny in `buildMeta` that silently changes member/public-profile privacy or unrelated product routes.
- **Links and members:** Keep normal navigation, related/cast links and member functions; give the retained cohort direct links from retained hubs. Do not make private data public or remove member content to hit a search count. Profiles/share lists keep their existing consent/visibility rules and their separate sitemap hold; record these as exceptions, so “100” means the experimental catalog cohort, not a false promise that no other public URL exists. No new robots block on catalog routes or removal of useful member UI.

**Risks:** Explicitly reverses the owner's 1,000-title/30-person/300-new-page scope. It would noindex at least **940 of the 1,000 current cohort titles**, at least **25 of 30 people**, and **290 of the 300 `/new` pages**, plus unselected catalog URLs outside XML. Two of the five fixed-sample people are outside the currently committed 30-person sitemap, so retaining the sample does not retain five members of that sitemap cohort. Those `/new` pages contain real regional value; lost eligibility could harm that future acquisition path, and reversal needs recrawls. People pages remain usable but lose most potential search landings. Public member exceptions mean this is not complete sitewide pruning. The intervention changes several route families and can introduce accidental noindex, stale XML or navigation regressions; implementation needs targeted checks including members and the render budget.

Crucially, a sitemap cut alone leaves other pages indexable. Conversely, **noindex is not a crawl-budget reallocation mechanism**: Google must fetch it, and blocking other pages does not create demand for the remaining ones. The experiment tests eligibility selection and perceived public value, not a promised hundred-way division of a fixed crawl quota. [Google's crawl guidance](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawl-budget).

**Time box and decision:** Eight weeks after deployment and Google's first read of the changed sitemap, with a **ten-week absolute limit from deployment** if that read is delayed. Preserve the original weekly record; add one inspection for each of the 100 retained URLs per week, total 150 with the original sample before deduplicating overlap. Measure titles, people, `/new` and hubs separately. Audit a rotating excluded sample within the same cap by reusing overlapping inspections, or on a separately budgeted read-only session. Confirm Google has actually seen exclusions before claiming a sitewide pruning test.

- **Continue:** At least **30/60 titles** stay indexed for two consecutive weeks, plus at least **two retained `/new` and two category/hub pages beyond `/`**, with non-home impressions. Hold 100 until traffic supports expansion; the existing #347 increase-by-250 rule still requires 70% of listed titles indexed.
- **Change course:** Crawls increase but pages remain excluded: crawl access/discovery improved, retained value did not. Gain restricted to `/new` suggests the differentiated arrival-history surface is worth testing instead of generic titles. A proportion gain caused only by a smaller denominator is not success; report absolute retained URL counts.
- **Stop:** At the absolute limit, fewer than **five non-home pages** retain indexing, or fewer than **20/60 titles** receive a fresh successful crawl. Take #359's hold/drop decision; distinguish insufficient exposure from fetched-page exclusion. Intermediate results mean hold the small cohort without further SEO expansion, not another automatic experiment. Keep utility pages online; decide any rollback for member/product value, not to make an indexing report look better.

### The channel decision remains separate
