## YepWatch follow-up — 2026-10-20, 11:38–11:43 UTC

### Preference context and honest naming assessment

**User-provided context:** Amity/unison/accord/nod did not click; screen/reel felt topical but insufficiently straightforward. Simple familiar words are preferred. A `.com` is optional, and the user does not regard the cited SwipeWatch app's small observed install bracket as a reason to discard the name. These are preferences for this naming investigation, not new repository decisions or standing agent instructions. No further unrelated shortlist is proposed.

**Judgment:** YepWatch improves on the earlier pool because neither word needs explaining to an English-speaking audience. “Yep” feels like the desired answer to a suggestion; “watch” gives a clearer everyday cue than “reel.” Pronunciation is naturally two beats, **“yep watch”**, and spelling is short. The weakness is that “yep” expresses one person's yes rather than everyone's agreement: the name alone does not promise matching, couples or groups. It works across couch, remote and mixed rooms without implying a particular location or synchronized playback.

In speech, “send me the YepWatch link” is usable, but a listener could hear a conversational “yep, watch,” write **Yep Watch**, substitute **Yup**, or miss the difference from another short affirmative brand. These are hypotheses for a spoken test, not observed error rates. Joined **YepWatch** is my proposed display form for easier recognition; spacing does not create a distinct spoken name. Outside a movie context, “watch” can mean a timepiece, while inside it the name could imply a streaming destination. Test whether a simple descriptor corrects those expectations before leaning on the name alone. Familiarity in English does not establish comprehension across GoodWatch's country coverage.
