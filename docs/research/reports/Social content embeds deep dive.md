# Embed official players, curate the URLs, discover elsewhere

As of 14 October 2026, GoodWatch can legally show real social content in two places: on title pages, and in a "Talked about now" start-page row that is the same for every visitor. It can do this by **embedding the platforms' own players and post widgets behind click-to-load consent facades**. It should not copy, re-host or scrape that content. Embedding is the easy half of the problem. YouTube (including Shorts), TikTok, Reddit, Bluesky, Mastodon, Twitch clips, Vimeo, Dailymotion and Snapchat all offer keyless embeds, and Instagram and Threads reportedly do again since June 2026. Discovery is the hard half: finding *which* posts are about a given title right now. Over the past decade every major platform has narrowed it. **Only YouTube still offers an open, keyword-searchable official API, and its default quota is about 100 searches a day.** TikTok and Instagram offer no legal discovery API for an app like GoodWatch. Reddit will close new API registrations on 31 Oct 2026 and remove remaining public API access by March 2027. X charges per read and sent cease-and-desist letters to no-auth readers in August 2026. The durable design follows from that. Compute a "talked about" signal from open data (Wikipedia pageviews, TMDB trending, Netflix Top 10, YouTube view velocity). Attach content from three channels: an allowlist of official YouTube channels, Bluesky and Mastodon, and a curated or editor-assisted URL queue for TikTok, Reels and Reddit. Render everything through one facade component that loads nothing from a third party until the visitor clicks. In Germany, that click-to-load pattern is not optional. Loading a YouTube or TikTok iframe on page load transmits the visitor's IP address and touches terminal storage without consent, which falls under §25 TDDDG and the post-2022 Google Fonts Abmahnung wave. The main residual legal risk is copyright exposure from "edit" accounts that post unlicensed film clips, which the EU's *GS Media* knowledge test attaches to the embedder once it is on notice.

The report has six main sections and three closing ones. It starts with how platform access closed over a decade. It then covers what embeds and what discovers today, the libraries, the wider set of communities beyond the big platforms, and the legal map with a Germany focus. It closes with a concrete architecture, a ranked source list, and the verification items the research sandbox could not settle.

**Evidence caveat that runs through everything below:** the research sandbox blocked direct fetches of most primary developer docs, terms pages and court texts (developers.google.com, developers.tiktok.com, developers.facebook.com, redditinc.com, docs.joinmastodon.org, wikidata.org, curia). Most findings come from search-result summaries of the cited pages. Items the notes flagged **[unverified]** keep that flag here. No endpoint was live-tested.

## Ten years of closing doors shaped what still works

The history matters because it predicts the future. Every platform has moved the same way. Open read APIs went first, then discovery, then metadata inside embeds. The embed itself, which carries the platform's branding, ads and link-back, has been the last thing standing everywhere.

**YouTube** has tightened by quota and by audit rather than by closing access. A Terms of Service revision took effect on 10 Feb 2017 ([TubePress](https://tubepress.com/blog/2017/02/dont-stress-about-the-updated-youtube-api-terms-of-service/)). From **1 Jul 2021**, any developer wanting more than 10,000 units a day had to pass an API Compliance Audit ([YouTube terms revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/revision-history)). The often-repeated story that the default quota fell from 50M to 1M units in 2016 and later to 10k could not be sourced this session **[unverified]**. Since 2014 the embedded player must keep a viewport of at least 200×200 px ([YouTube player parameters](https://developers.google.com/youtube/player_parameters?csw=1)). Shorts grew to a 3-minute maximum on 15 Oct 2024 ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/10/03/youtube-shorts-3-minutes/)). YouTube retired its Trending page around 21 Jul 2025 and replaced it with category charts, including **"Trending Movie Trailers"** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/10/youtube-is-getting-rid-of-its-trending-page-and-trending-now-list)). Late in 2025 it began rejecting embeds that send no `Referer` header, with **Error 153** ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Dec/1/youtube-embed-153-error/)). In parallel, Proof-of-Origin tokens and SABR-only streaming made yt-dlp-style access steadily more expensive ([yt-dlp PO Token Guide](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/PO-Token-Guide)).

**Meta** is the clearest case of an embed losing its metadata. Instagram and Facebook oEmbed required tokens from 23–24 Oct 2020 ([Meta dev blog](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2020/10/14/required-migration-token-based-access-user-picture-oEmbed-endpoints/)). Instagram Basic Display died on **4 Dec 2024** ([Meta docs](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/docs/instagram-basic-display-api)). The "Meta oEmbed Read" feature replaced the old one on 8 Apr 2025. Meta then removed `thumbnail_url`, `author_name` and `author_url` from Facebook and Instagram oEmbed responses, finally on **3 Nov 2025** ([Meta changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/changelog/non-versioned-changes/nvc-2025); [Iframely](https://iframely.com/updates/193071-facebook-and-instagram-oembed-thumbnail-deprecation)). API Evangelist called the change hostile to attribution ([API Evangelist](https://apievangelist.com/2025/04/09/why-would-meta-remove-oembed-author-fields/index.md)). Then came a reversal. Several WordPress-ecosystem sources report that from **15 Jun 2026** Instagram, Facebook and Threads oEmbed can again be called **without a token or App Review**, and a developer test on 11 Sep 2026 confirmed it worked. The behaviour is undocumented and "rate limits on tokenless calls may differ" ([WP Mayor](https://wpmayor.com/meta-tokenless-oembed-wordpress/); [Feedframer](https://feedframer.com/guides/instagram-post-embed)). Meta's own WordPress plugin advertises "tokenless oEmbed APIs… No access tokens" ([facebook/meta-embeds-for-wordpress](https://github.com/facebook/meta-embeds-for-wordpress)). Treat this as real but revocable **[unverified against Meta's primary docs]**. This updates the earlier first-pass report, which still assumed App Review was required.

**Reddit** went from the most open platform to one of the most closed in three years. API pricing at **$0.24 per 1,000 calls** took effect on 1 Jul 2023 ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/whatis/feature/Reddit-pricing-API-charge-explained)). Apollo shut down after estimating a bill of about $20M a year ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/23/05/31/the-best-reddit-client-app-is-in-danger-due-to-price-demands)), and 6,000+ subreddits went dark on 12 Jun 2023 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2023/06/12/reddit-blackout-api-charges-prompt-community-outrage-shutdowns.html)). Pushshift lost its privileged access in 2023 ([THINKPOL](https://think-pol.com/pushshift-alternative)). The **Responsible Builder Policy** reportedly took effect around 11 Nov 2025 and requires explicit approval before *any* API access, hobby projects included ([Reddit Help](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/42728983564564-Responsible-Builder-Policy); [ReplyDaddy](https://replydaddy.com/blog/reddit-api-pre-approval-2025-personal-projects-crackdown)). Anonymous `.json` endpoints have returned 403 since late May 2026 ([Crawlora](https://crawlora.net/blog/reddit-json-api-blocked-2026)). On **30 Sep 2026** Reddit announced the end of RSS on **13 Nov 2026** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/reddit-is-killing-rss-feeds-ending-public-api-access-because-of-ai-bots/); [AndroidHeadlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/10/reddit-is-ending-rss-feeds-amid-scraping-concerns.html)). Secondary coverage says new public Data API registrations close on **31 Oct 2026** and remaining external access ends by **March 2027**, with apps steered to the Devvit Developer Platform ([Lapaas Voice](https://lapaasvoice.com/reddit-to-shut-down-rss-feeds-in-november-public-api-access-by-march-2027); [Reddit changelog 8 Oct 2026](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/54353370049684-Changelog-October-8-2026)). Behind all this sit AI licensing deals, reportedly about $60M a year with Google and about $70M a year with OpenAI ([LLM Pulse](https://llmpulse.ai/blog/ai-content-licensing-deals/)). Reddit's data now has a price, and free readers are the leak it is plugging.

**X** walled off logged-out viewing in June 2023 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/twitter-isnt-showing-tweets-unless-youre-logged-in-165254006.html)). It made pay-per-use the default API pricing on **6 Feb 2026**, at about $0.005 per post read ([postproxy](https://postproxy.dev/blog/x-api-pricing-2026/)). On **24 Aug 2026** it sent cease-and-desist letters citing the Texas Harmful Access by Computer Act and the Lanham Act, which took nitter.net and XCancel offline ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nitter-xcancel-offline-x-cease-and-desist); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/personal-tech/2026/09/07/nitter-lives-to-proxy-another-day-after-taking-legal-advice/5294754)). **TikTok** never opened discovery at all. Its Research API is limited to non-profit academic researchers in the US, EEA, UK and Switzerland, and even a European research institute was refused ([TikTok](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/about-research-api); [KInIT](https://kinit.sk/when-research-apis-close-the-door/)). Critics call it an "illusion" of access ([Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/unpacking-tiktoks-data-access-illusion)). **Twitch** shut down Kraken v5 on 28 Feb 2022 ([Barry Carlyon](https://barrycarlyon.co.uk/wordpress/2022/02/)). **Snapchat** moved the other way and launched web embeds for Spotlight on 16 Oct 2023 ([Snap Newsroom](https://newsroom.snap.com/how-to-embed-snapchat-content)). The ecosystem around film and TV also contracted. **Google ended the third-party Tenor API on 30 Jun 2026** ([Techgenyz](https://techgenyz.com/google-shut-down-tenor-api-gif-pickers/)). **TV Time shut down on 15 Jul 2026** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/02/popular-tv-tracking-app-tv-time-is-shutting-down-as-company-focuses-on-ai)). Trakt reportedly began requiring VIP for new developer API apps around 31 Jul 2026 ([tuliprox #853](https://github.com/euzu/tuliprox/issues/853)) **[unverified against a primary Trakt source]**. Bluesky's unauthenticated `searchPosts` now returns 403 ([bluesky-mcp-server #32](https://github.com/cyanheads/bluesky-mcp-server/issues/32)).

## Nine platforms still embed with a plain iframe and no key

What *can* go on a GoodWatch page today is broader than the history suggests, because embeds survived the closures. YouTube Shorts have no dedicated vertical embed. Rewrite `/shorts/{id}` to `/embed/{id}` and place it in a 9:16 box (`aspect-ratio: 9/16`, max width about 320 px). Putting a `/shorts/` URL straight into an iframe can fail ([Flockler](https://flockler.com/blog/embed-youtube-shorts); [Smash Balloon](https://smashballoon.com/youtube-shorts-embed-not-working/)). The player needs a viewport of at least 200×200 px, and Google recommends 480×270 for 16:9 ([YouTube RMF](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality)). Since late 2025 the page must send a Referer. `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` produces Error 153, so `strict-origin-when-cross-origin` is the safe setting ([UMich KB](https://teamdynamix.umich.edu/TDClient/30/Portal/KB/Article/14491/Fixing-YouTube-Player-Error-153-with-Referrer-Policy-Settings)). In the repo, the only `no-referrer` header found is on the search-busy 503 JSON response in `goodwatch-webapp/app/server/search-runtime/admission.server.ts`, which does not affect pages. No explicit site-wide policy is set, so browsers fall back to `strict-origin-when-cross-origin`. Setting that policy explicitly would protect against a future hardening change.

**TikTok** has a proper embed player at `https://www.tiktok.com/player/v1/{post_id}`. It takes query parameters (`autoplay` defaults to 0, `rel` to 1, plus `controls`, `music_info` and `description`) and has a postMessage API: the host can send `play` and `seekTo`, and the player emits `onPlayerReady`, `onStateChange`, `onCurrentTime` and `onMute` ([TikTok Embed Player](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/embed-player)). This corrects the folklore, repeated in older libraries, that TikTok embeds cannot be controlled. TikTok's oEmbed takes only a `url` and lists no key. It returns a `thumbnail_url` and the legacy blockquote plus `embed.js` HTML ([TikTok Embed Videos](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/embed-videos)). The thumbnail URLs carry expiring signatures and commonly die within days ([Iframely](https://iframely.com/help/015914-how-to-deal-with-expiring-images-of-tik-tok-and-instagram)). The legacy embed is heavy: about 500 KB of JavaScript and 8–12 MB on the wire per embed in 2022 measurements ([Justin Ribeiro](https://justinribeiro.com/chronicle/2022/07/15/terrible-tiktok-embed-web-performance-and-my-imperfect-web-component-solution/)), and a page with 18 of them crashed mobile browsers ([WordPress/performance #1706](https://github.com/WordPress/performance/issues/1706)).

**Instagram Reels** embed through the `instagram-media` blockquote plus Instagram's `embed.js`, which builds the iframe client-side. XenForo and react-ig-embed implement Reels this way without touching oEmbed ([XenForo](https://xenforo.com/community/threads/203995); [react-ig-embed](https://github.com/justinmahar/react-ig-embed)). No official documentation exists for a direct `instagram.com/reel/{id}/embed` iframe, and remixes and collab posts often have embedding disabled ([Shopify community](https://community.shopify.com/t/instagram-reel-embed-issue/398544)). **Facebook video** embeds through `facebook.com/plugins/video.php?href=…` ([Meta Embedded Video Player](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/plugins/embedded-video-player/)). Whether Facebook *Reels* URLs work there is **[unverified]**. **Twitch clips** need a raw iframe at `clips.twitch.tv/embed?clip={slug}&parent={hostname}`. The `parent` value must exactly match the serving host, so goodwatch.app and any preview domains must each be listed ([Twitch docs](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/embed/video-and-clips/)). **Snapchat Spotlight** is script-only: a blockquote with `data-snapchat-embed-url` plus `snapchat.com/embed.js` ([Snap developer docs](https://developers.snap.com/api/snapchat-for-web/social-plugins/embedding-web-content)). **Vimeo** oEmbed needs no token, but owners can domain-restrict videos ([Vimeo Help](https://help.vimeo.com/hc/en-us/articles/12427906892689-Use-oEmbed-with-private-videos)). **Dailymotion** moved legacy embeds onto `geo.dailymotion.com/player.html` with a default player from 1 Sep 2025, and most old parameters stopped working ([Dailymotion migration guide](https://developers.dailymotion.com/migration-guide-new-embed-endpoint)). **Bilibili, Niconico and Rumble** iframes exist for international content ([Discourse Bilibili](https://meta.discourse.org/t/discourse-embed-bilibili/282491); [Iframely Niconico](https://iframely.com/domains/niconico); [deepakness](https://deepakness.com/raw/rumble-embed-option/)).

Text networks embed too. **Reddit's** official flow is Share → Embed via publish.reddit.com. If the post is later edited or deleted, the embed shows a notice ([Reddit Help](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043033532-How-do-I-embed-a-Reddit-post-or-comment-in-an-article-or-other-publication)). The historical `reddit.com/oembed` lacked CORS and had to be called server-side ([joshuatz](https://joshuatz.com/posts/2019/reddit-apis-and-building-reddit-embeds/)). Nothing found says embeds are affected by the 2026 shutdowns, but they should be re-tested after 13 Nov 2026. **Bluesky** posts embed through `embed.bsky.app` and its oEmbed **[unverified 2026 format]**. **Mastodon** exposes `/api/oembed`, which returns an iframe of `/@user/:id/embed` **[unverified]**. **Threads** embeds through Meta's tokenless oEmbed, as described above. **X** posts can embed through the official `widgets.js` blockquote **[unverified 2026 behaviour]**. Its official embed was described as broken in August 2025 ([samwize](https://samwize.com/2025/08/10/the-x-com-embed-disaster-still-broken-but-we-have-a-reverse-engineered-solution/)), and logged-out visitors see single posts only, never replies ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2023/07/05/twitter-users-without-accounts-can-see-tweets-not-profiles-replies/)).

## Finding the right post is where every platform says no

Discovery means answering "which posts are about *Severance* this week?" Here the platforms diverge sharply, and this, not embedding, decides the architecture. **YouTube Data API v3** is the only open, official keyword discovery. The default is **10,000 units a day**, and `search.list` costs 100 units, which works out to about 100 searches. More quota requires a compliance audit ([Google: quota and audits](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/guides/quota_and_compliance_audits?authuser=6)). One source reports a separate search bucket, which Google has not confirmed ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-data-api/)). The cheap path avoids search entirely. `playlistItems.list` on a channel's upload playlist costs 1 unit ([Google quota cost](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost)), and channel RSS feeds return the latest ~15 uploads for free, although they have been intermittently flaky since Dec 2025 ([NewsBlur forum](https://forum.newsblur.com/t/youtube-feeds-broken/13227)). `videos.list?chart=mostPopular` with category 1 (Film & Animation), 30 (Movies) or 44 (Trailers) returns a regional popularity chart ([Google guide](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/guides/implementation/videos?hl=en)). Whether the July 2025 Trending removal changed this endpoint is unknown. YouTube statistics fetched as non-authorized data may be stored for **at most 30 days**, unless the developer is accepted into the derived-metrics program, which allows views and likes for 36 months ([derived-metrics policy](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/derived-metrics-policy)). Any view-velocity "hotness" calculation has to live within that window.

**TikTok** offers no legitimate discovery to an app like GoodWatch. The Display API covers only an authenticated user's own videos ([Scrapfly](https://scrapfly.io/blog/guide-to-tiktok-api)). Creative Center trending-hashtag JSON reportedly returns only 3 hashtags per unauthenticated call and gets IP-blocked ([Apify](https://apify.com/ayen-data/tiktok-trending-hashtags-scraper.md)). yt-dlp's TikTok extractor broke in Jan 2026 and was fixed again in the 2026.08.19 release ([yt-dlp #15506](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/15506); [yt-dlp releases](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/releases)). **Instagram** has no hashtag or keyword discovery for non-business apps. Graph API hashtag search reportedly requires a business account and allows 30 unique hashtags per 7 days **[unverified]**. Its web JSON (`web_profile_info` with an `x-ig-app-id` header) is ToS-prohibited and drifts ([Scrapfly](https://scrapfly.io/blog/how-to-scrape-instagram/)). **Threads** keyword search requires the `threads_keyword_search` permission through App Review ([Meta docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/threads/keyword-search/)). The notes cite a cap of 500 queries per 7 days from a ~22-month-old article ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/meta-introduces-new-search-and-analytics-features-to-threads-api-platform/)). The earlier first-pass report cited 2,200 queries per 24 hours. The two figures conflict and both need checking. **X** charges about $0.005 per read ([outstand.so](https://www.outstand.so/blog/x-api-pricing)), so 1,000 title searches returning 20 posts each costs roughly $100. The only unofficial route, the `cdn.syndication.twimg.com/tweet-result` endpoint used by Vercel's react-tweet, returns single known tweets, not search ([react-tweet fetch-tweet.ts](https://github.com/vercel/react-tweet/blob/main/packages/react-tweet/src/api/fetch-tweet.ts)). **Twitch** Helix Get Clips works with an app token, filtered by broadcaster or game and a date range ([Twitch API](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/api/clips/)). Its `game_id` model fits games, not films, so it serves watch-along and premiere streams only.

**Reddit** discovery now has to come from outside Reddit. An application filed today would race the 31 Oct 2026 registration close, for access that ends by March 2027 anyway. Three paths remain. The first is a web search API restricted to `site:reddit.com/r/movies` and similar subreddits. Google's own Reddit deal and its "Discussions and forums" block make search engines the de facto Reddit index ([TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/27/googles-new-tools-help-discussion-forums-and-social-media-platforms-rank-higher-in-search-results)). The second is editorial curation of predictable megathreads, such as r/movies "Official Discussion" and r/television episode threads. The third is Arctic Shift, a free, keyless community archive that lags 4–6 weeks behind and is useless for "now" ([redreplier](https://redreplier.com/blog/arctic-shift)). Using SerpApi for the search route carries a specific 2026 risk: Reddit's §1201 claims against SerpApi and Perplexity survived dismissal ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/416950/reddit-can-proceed-with-scraping-claims-against-pe.html)). An official search API such as Brave's or Bing's is cleaner, though the notes do not cover their pricing.

**Bluesky** and **Mastodon** are the open exceptions. Bluesky runs four official, unauthenticated **Jetstream** JSON firehose instances ([Jaz's blog](https://jazco.dev/2024/09/24/jetstream/)). A small consumer can filter every public post for title names, hashtags (#FilmSky, #TVSky) or links to IMDb, TMDB and Letterboxd. Authenticated `searchPosts` works with an app-password session from a GoodWatch bot account ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/ethan_teague/how-to-scrape-bluesky-data-in-2026-posts-profiles-and-search-1a2k)). Mastodon's `/api/v1/trends/tags` and `/api/v1/trends/statuses` are public, admin-moderated, and exclude content-warned, sensitive and reply posts. They are cached for 15 minutes ([fedibird PR #186](https://github.com/fedibird/mastodon/pull/186); [bentasker](https://www.bentasker.co.uk/posts/blog/security/restricting-unauthenticated-access-to-mastodons-public-feeds.html)). Full-text search respects the account-level `indexable` flag ([mastodon #28674](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/discussions/28674)), and fediverse norms are hostile to aggregation. GoodWatch should read only trends and tag timelines from a handful of large instances, skip accounts marked non-discoverable or `#nobot`, and embed rather than copy.

Hosted scraping vendors fill the TikTok and Reels gap cheaply. **ScrapeCreators** sells credits that never expire at about **$1.88 per 1,000 calls**. A weekly keyword search across 500 titles would cost about $4 a month ([CreatorCrawl](https://creatorcrawl.com/alternatives/scrapecreators/)). EnsembleData starts at $100 a month ([EnsembleData pricing](https://ensembledata.com/pricing)), and Apify's TikTok actor costs $1–3 per 1,000 results ([SocialCrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/best-tiktok-data-apis-2026)). Most of these comparison pages come from competing vendors. More importantly, no vendor gives GoodWatch a licence. The platform's terms still forbid the use. In the EU, bulk-collecting personal data this way runs into the Dutch DPA's position that private scraping is "almost always" unlawful ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/netherlands-dpa-issues-guidance-against-web-scraping)). A defensible use is narrow: vendor search produces *candidate URLs* for an editor to review. GoodWatch stores only the post ID and URL, never the payload, and the final display goes through the official embed.

Matching posts to titles is a separate problem. No maintained open-source "movie mention" linker exists, and the spaCy linkers date from 2023 ([PyPI spacy-entity-linker](https://pypi.org/project/spacy-entity-linker/)). Practitioners use a four-step pipeline. They normalize titles, retrieve TMDB candidates, and accept a match only above a score threshold *and* a margin over the runner-up ([relos #19](https://github.com/relos-tv/relos/issues/19)). Ambiguous cases go to an LLM with the candidate list and a "none" option. LLM linking has measurably improved title extraction from noisy Reddit text ([arXiv 2502.14137](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14137)). GoodWatch already has the pieces: Qdrant for embedding similarity, the OpenRouter integration in `goodwatch-flows/windmill/f/dna/`, and Wikidata links in `f/external_ids/`. Official handles are the high-precision first pass. TMDB title `external_ids` include Facebook, Instagram and Twitter IDs ([TMDB movie external IDs](https://developer.themoviedb.org/reference/movie-external-ids)). TikTok and YouTube IDs are confirmed only on *person* records ([TMDB person external IDs](https://developer.themoviedb.org/reference/person-external-ids)). The earlier claim that title records carry `tiktok_id` is therefore **[unverified]**. Wikidata adds subreddit (P3984), hashtag (P2572) and social usernames, all with **[unverified]** property numbers.

## Maintained libraries cover YouTube and text, not TikTok or Instagram

The frontend toolkit is uneven. For YouTube, `react-lite-youtube-embed` 3.7.0 (Sep 2026, MIT) and Paul Irish's `lite-youtube-embed` 0.3.4 (Nov 2025) are maintained facades ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/react-lite-youtube-embed); [npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/lite-youtube-embed)). Lighthouse's own example puts the facade at **3 KB against 540 KB** for the player ([Chrome Lighthouse](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/third-party-facades)). That is a facade in spirit, but the click is not tied to any consent record. For tweets, Vercel's `react-tweet` 3.3.1 (Jun 2026) renders statically with no iframe, but it depends on X's unofficial syndication endpoint, which its own maintainers say "can break at anytime" ([react-tweet #76](https://github.com/vercel/react-tweet/issues/76)). For Bluesky, `bsky-react-post` and `react-bluesky-embed` render posts statically and work with React Server Components, but they are small hobby projects ([bsky-react-post](https://bsky-react-post.rhinobase.io/)). **No maintained "lite" component exists for TikTok, Instagram or Mastodon.** `react-tiktok` dates from 2020 and `react-instagram-embed` from 2021 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/react-tiktok); [npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/react-instagram-embed)). GoodWatch should therefore write one small facade component of its own rather than adopt five wrappers.

For metadata, `@extractus/oembed-extractor` 6.0.0 (Aug 2026), `open-graph-scraper` 6.12.0 and `metascraper` 5.58.4 (Oct 2026) are all MIT and active, which makes hosted proxies unnecessary ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@extractus/oembed-extractor); [npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/metascraper)). Iframely's free tier is 2,000 "hits" a month, where a hit is one active URL-hour ([Iframely pricing](https://iframely.com/pricing)). That suits one-off resolution in a pipeline but not live rendering. For consent, orestbida's `vanilla-cookieconsent` 3.1.0 (Feb 2025) with **iframemanager** gives per-vendor placeholders with custom thumbnails ([iframemanager](https://github.com/orestbida/iframemanager)). Klaro's contextual consent does the same, but its last npm release was in Mar 2024 and it ignores elements injected after init, which is a poor fit for a React SPA ([klaro #434](https://github.com/kiprotect/klaro/issues/434)). Heise's server-proxied `@heise/embetty-base` has not been released since 2019 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@heise/embetty-base)). Google Consent Mode v2 is irrelevant unless GoodWatch runs Google ad or analytics tags ([Consently](https://consently.net/blog/cookiebot-review)). GoodWatch's current `goodwatch-webapp/app/ui/CookieConsent.tsx` stores one global yes/no. A per-vendor model can sit beside it, with CookieConsent v3 providing the state and a GoodWatch React facade reading it.

On the Python side of the Windmill pipelines, these are healthy in 2026: `google-api-python-client` 2.201.0, `atproto` 0.0.72 (with Firehose and Jetstream), `Mastodon.py` 2.2.2, `feedparser` 6.0.14, `trafilatura` 2.3.1, and `praw` 8.0.3 for anyone who already holds Reddit credentials ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/atproto/); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/praw/)). ## German law makes consent the gate and edit accounts the hazard

The legal picture divides into four layers, and each one points to the same design.

**Copyright of embedding.** In the EU, embedding a freely available work that its rightsholder uploaded is not a communication to a "new public" (*Svensson* C-466/12; *BestWater* C-348/13) ([curia](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-348/13)). The BGH followed this for YouTube embeds in *Die Realität II* (2015) **[unverified]**. The hazard is the *unlicensed* upload. Under *GS Media* (C-160/15), linking to infringing content infringes if the linker knew or should have known. Knowledge is **presumed for links posted "for profit"** ([curia](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-160/15)). LG Hamburg (2016) read "for profit" broadly, as covering any generally commercial site **[unverified]**. That makes GoodWatch's non-commercial status a real legal asset, provided it is operationalized as no ads, no affiliate links and no sponsorships. Rightsholders then carry the burden of proving knowledge, typically through a notice, which GoodWatch must act on promptly. Edit accounts that crop, speed up or mirror film clips to evade matching are the concrete risk ([Syracuse Law Review](https://lawreview.syr.edu/scene-stealers-the-legal-risks-of-posting-movies-on-tiktok)). *VG Bild-Kunst* (C-392/19) adds that framing around technical anti-framing measures is infringing ([curia](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-392/19)). If a creator disables embedding, GoodWatch must not proxy around it. *Renckhoff* (C-161/17) holds that saving a copy and re-serving it is a new communication ([curia](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-161/17)). Caching thumbnails or avatars on GoodWatch's servers is therefore reproduction, not embedding. The US is split. The Ninth Circuit's server test, reaffirmed in *Hunley v. Instagram* (2023), protects embedders ([DWT](https://dwt.com/insights/2023/08/server-test-instagram-case-ninth-circuit)). SDNY courts in *Goldman v. Breitbart* and the Instagram-licence cases (*Sinclair*, *McGucken*) did not follow it **[unverified details]**. Practical US exposure is mostly photo-licensing claims over embedded posts that contain photos.

**Privacy and terminal access.** This is the binding German constraint. §25 TDDDG requires consent for storing or reading information on the device unless it is strictly necessary. The DSK's guidance treats video add-ons as wanted only when the visitor actively uses them ([datenkodex.de](https://datenkodex.de/wissen/was-gilt-bei-eingebetteten-youtube-videos-und-social-media-plugins)). LG München I (3 O 17493/20, Jan 2022) awarded €100 for transmitting a visitor's IP address to Google through a dynamically loaded font, which started a wave of Abmahnungen (warning letters) ([dr-dsgvo.de](https://dr-dsgvo.de/youtube-videos-und-die-dsgvo/)). *Fashion ID* (C-40/17) makes the embedding site a joint controller for the transmission ([curia](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-40/17)). German practitioners agree that **youtube-nocookie.com still requires consent**, because it still connects to Google and uses storage ([dr-dsgvo.de](https://dr-dsgvo.de/youtube-plugin-selbst-mit-nocookie-zusatz-werden-cookies-gesetzt-einwilligung-immer-notwendig/)). Damages case law is mixed. The BGH (VI ZR 109/23, Jan 2025) requires concrete damage beyond the breach itself ([MIR](https://medien-internet-und-recht.de/volltext.php?mir_dok_id=3453)), while LG Leipzig awarded €5,000 against Meta's tracking tools in 2025 ([rewis](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/hiy-04-07-2025-05-o-235123/)). No German court has ruled specifically on social-post embeds without consent. The facade pattern resolves this. Server-side rendering posts as static HTML avoids §25, but it moves the problem. GoodWatch then processes the author's personal data, which needs a legitimate-interest assessment and an Art. 14 notice approach. It also makes copies (*Renckhoff*), and it likely breaches X's and Meta's display rules **[lawyer]**.

**Press snippets.** Art. 15 DSM and §§87f–87k UrhG leave hyperlinks and "individual words or very short extracts" free. Germany never enacted a fixed length, and the first CJEU case on the right (*Like Company v Google*, C-250/25) had a hearing on 10 Mar 2026 but no judgment yet ([CREATe](https://www.create.ac.uk/blog/2026/03/03/copyright-and-ai-case-c-250-25-like-company-before-the-grand-chamber-of-the-court-of-justice-of-the-european-union-opinion-by-the-european-copyright-society/)). Headline, source, date and link are safe. A lead sentence plus a thumbnail is gray. The exemption for non-commercial use by *individual users* does not cover a public web app **[lawyer]**.

**Scraping, platform terms and the DSA.** In the US, logged-out scraping of public data largely survived contract claims (*Meta v. Bright Data*, 2024 **[unverified]**). *X v. Bright Data* held contract claims preempted by copyright ([Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/judge-tosses-xs-contract-claims-against-data-scraping-company/)). But *Reddit v. Anthropic* was remanded on 30 Mar 2026 on the opposite preemption reasoning ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/northern-district-of-california-court-holds-state-tort-and-contract-claims-not-preempted-by-federal-copyright-act-remands-reddit-v-anthropic-to-state-court)). The live 2026 theory is **DMCA §1201 anti-circumvention**: Reddit's claims against SerpApi and Perplexity survived on 31 Jul 2026 ([Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc)), while Google's parallel claims were dismissed on 20 Jul 2026 ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/court-dismisses-googles-dmca-claims-against-serpapi/583033/)). Defeating anti-bot measures with CAPTCHA solving or rotating residential proxies now carries real US litigation risk. In the EU, the dominant scraping risk is the GDPR, not contract. Machine-readable opt-outs bind TDM after OLG Hamburg's *LAION* ruling (Dec 2025, now before the BGH) ([Vossius](https://www.vossius.eu/de/news/detail/urteil-des-olg-hamburg-zu-ki-trainingsdatensaetzen)), and TDM never covers *display*. Under the DSA, a site that only embeds content it selected itself is probably not a hosting service. If it accepts user-submitted links that it stores and shows, it becomes one and owes notice-and-action (Art. 16) and statements of reasons (Art. 17). Small enterprises are exempt from most extra platform duties ([EUR-Lex DSA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj)) **[unverified article-level detail]**. A German Impressum (§5 DDG / §18 MStV) is needed regardless **[unverified]**.

| Risk tier | Practices |
|---|---|
| Low | Plain links; official, unmodified embeds of rightsholder uploads loaded after consent; headline + source + link; official APIs within terms |
| Gray | Embeds of edit/fan accounts; headline + lead sentence + thumbnail; cached or proxied thumbnails/avatars; server-rendered posts; vendor-scraped discovery; "non-commercial" with donations |
| High | Circumventing anti-bot tech; logged-in scraping; bulk personal-data scraping in the EU; proxying around disabled embedding; re-hosting media; iframes before consent in Germany; overlays on players |

## Recommended architecture: signal first, IDs only, one consent facade

The architecture has four layers. Each maps onto existing GoodWatch seams.

**Layer 1, the signal (which titles are hot).** Add a Windmill flow, for example `f/buzz/`, following the `next` → `fetch` pattern and the polite fetching in `goodwatch-flows/windmill/f/critic_sites/polite_http.py`. It writes a daily row per title with Wikimedia pageviews (via the Wikidata links already in `f/external_ids/`), TMDB trending and popularity delta, Netflix Top 10 rank, and YouTube view velocity from two snapshots within the 30-day storage window. Score each title as a z-score against its own 28-day baseline. That ranks "unusually discussed" rather than "big", and the daily table also settles the backlog's "trending per day / yesterday / difference" items. The "Talked about now" row reads the top N from this table. It is identical for every visitor, so it fits the anonymous page cache in `goodwatch-webapp/app/server/living-room/start-titles.server.ts` and the row slot in `goodwatch-webapp/app/ui/living-room/BelowRoom.tsx`.

**Layer 2, content discovery (which posts belong to those titles).** A `f/social/` flow runs source adapters that each emit the same candidate record: `platform`, `post_id`, `canonical_url`, `creator_handle`, `published_at`, `tmdb_id`, `link_confidence`, `trust_tier`, `spoiler`, `nsfw`, `discovered_via`. The YouTube adapter reads upload playlists or RSS of an **allowlist of official studio, distributor and streamer channels** (tier 1) at 1 unit per call. It also spends `search.list` only on the top ~50 buzz titles a day, and treats a video as a Short when it runs 3 minutes or less and is vertical. The Bluesky adapter is a long-running Jetstream consumer, plus authenticated `searchPosts` from a bot account. The Mastodon adapter polls trends and tag timelines on a few instances and enforces opt-out flags. The news adapter uses feedparser on trade RSS every ~30 minutes and stores headline, source, time and URL only. The **curation queue** covers TikTok, Instagram Reels, Threads, Reddit and Twitch. An editor pastes or approves URLs, and optional low-volume candidate suggestions come from a web search API (`site:reddit.com`, `site:tiktok.com`) or, if the user accepts the terms risk, a vendor such as ScrapeCreators, used only to *suggest* URLs. Entity linking runs in four stages: official-handle match, then fuzzy title plus year, then Qdrant similarity, then an OpenRouter LLM with a "none" option. Store **IDs and URLs plus GoodWatch's own fields**, not post payloads. Copy them through the usual MongoDB → Crate path. A **deletion-sync job** re-resolves each item's oEmbed every few days and drops 404s. That honours the terms (X, TikTok and Meta all require removing deleted content) and the GDPR, and it ages YouTube data out within 30 days.

**Layer 3, rendering (one facade component).** Build a single `<SocialEmbed platform=… id=…>` in the webapp. It server-renders a plain-HTML card: platform badge, creator handle, GoodWatch's own label (for example "Official trailer · Netflix" or "Discussion · r/television"), and a "Load from TikTok — data goes to TikTok" button. It loads nothing from the third party: no preconnect, no dns-prefetch. On click, it checks per-vendor consent and then mounts the **unmodified official embed**. That means YouTube `/embed/{id}` on youtube-nocookie in a 16:9 or 9:16 box no smaller than 200×200, TikTok `player/v1/{id}`, Reddit's embed, Bluesky's embed.bsky.app, Instagram's blockquote plus embed.js, Twitch with the `parent` list, or Spotify's episode iframe. GoodWatch's own ratings and badges go beside the player, never over it. Only one live player is mounted at a time. The existing `YoutubePlayer.tsx` click-to-load should become one branch of this component. Set `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin` site-wide.

The thumbnail question has no clean answer and needs a lawyer. Privacy guidance favours a locally hosted preview ([datenkodex.de](https://datenkodex.de/wissen/was-gilt-bei-eingebetteten-youtube-videos-und-social-media-plugins)). Copyright (*Renckhoff*) treats a stored copy as reproduction. TikTok thumbnails expire, and Meta returns none. Two options exist until a lawyer settles it. The safest default is a **text-and-brand facade with no third-party image**. The middle option is a **transient, short-TTL image proxy** limited to tier-1 official uploads, whose legal status under the §44a temporary-copy exception is untested. Title pages load the section as a below-fold extra through `goodwatch-webapp/app/server/title-extras.server.ts` with `cached()`, and register it in `goodwatch-webapp/app/ui/details/sections.ts`.

**Layer 4, consent and governance.** Add a per-vendor consent manager. `vanilla-cookieconsent` v3 can supply state for YouTube, TikTok, Meta, Reddit, Bluesky, Twitch and Spotify, alongside or replacing the binary state in `goodwatch-webapp/app/ui/CookieConsent.tsx`. The facade click counts as the consent action, with an optional "always load from TikTok" toggle. Write down "non-commercial" as no ads, affiliate links or sponsorships. That preserves TMDB's terms and the *GS Media* burden of proof. Exclude tier-3 edit accounts and "full movie in parts" uploads from the curation queue. Every card gets a report or hide link and a takedown contact, which is useful for *GS Media* notices whether or not the DSA applies. Hide anything flagged spoiler, NSFW, content-warned or Bluesky `!no-unauthenticated`. Behind a spoiler reveal, also hide episode discussion threads younger than a few days. Before launch, hand a German IT or media lawyer four questions: thumbnail handling, server-rendered text posts, the embedding of fan uploads, and DSA status if users can submit links.

## Conclusion

The earlier first pass treated Reddit, X and Threads applications as the durable long-term option. The new evidence reverses that. Official API access is what is disappearing: Reddit closes new registrations within weeks, X sues the people who read it without paying, and Trakt and Tenor gated or shut down their APIs this year. The embed is what every platform keeps, because it carries their brand, ads and link-back. Meta even restored keyless embeds after five years of tokens. GoodWatch should therefore treat the *embed* as the stable interface and *discovery* as the replaceable part. Every source then reduces to one question: how does GoodWatch get a trustworthy post URL for this title? Official YouTube channels, Bluesky's firehose and an editor's judgment answer it legally today. Everything else answers it at a cost in terms risk, money or maintenance.

Two less obvious implications follow. First, non-commercial status is worth more legally than it first appears. It keeps TMDB's licence and also shifts the *GS Media* burden of proof onto rightsholders. Any future monetisation decision is therefore also a copyright-risk decision for every embedded fan clip. Second, the German consent rule improves the product rather than just constraining it. A facade that ships zero third-party bytes until the visitor clicks is also what makes a cacheable, fast, identical-for-everyone "Talked about now" row possible. Compliance and performance call for the same component, so building it first is the highest-leverage step.
