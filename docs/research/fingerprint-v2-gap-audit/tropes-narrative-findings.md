# Narrative, relationship and character gaps from stored tropes

## Evidence and scope

Primary sources: [exact-name inventory](tropes-inventory-all.json), [stored URL inventory](tropes-url-inventory.json), [stored title-specific explanations](tropes-stored-examples.json), [existing recommendation set](recommended-changes.json), [compact V2 definition](../../design/fingerprint-v2/llm-definition.json), [full V2 definition](../../design/fingerprint-v2/definition.json). No live TV Tropes page was requested; [repository rules](../../tvtropes.md) prohibit agent web-reading of those pages.

The shared inventory contains 42,936 exact name strings. This branch examined the 450 most frequent labels, focused name families and their available stored explanation snippets. It is a targeted semantic audit, not a claim to have manually interpreted all names. Counts below are distinct catalog IDs per exact label, as exported; they are neither verified distinct works nor observed search demand. Different labels can occur on the same title, and alias variants must not be added together blindly.

Stored explanations are not canonical general definitions. Their titles and scopes can be unreliable: a movie-labelled The Wall source discusses an album, a Twilight source discusses books, and a Fame example discusses a season finale. Some explanations repeat under different catalog IDs. Treat these as evidence of vocabulary/application semantics, then verify work identity and scope before production annotation.

## Main recommendation

**Foreshadowing/setup-payoff and dramatic irony are the strongest additional broad storytelling candidates beyond the previous recommendation set.** Add them to scoring evaluation, not automatically to the required generation vector. Most other findings need sparse structure concepts or character/event relationships. A much larger fixed vector would repeatedly count the same information and obscure whether a tag is asserted, subverted, or merely discussed.

A setup/payoff relation is especially useful: Chekhov’s Gun is about an early detail becoming consequential later. It is not a synonym for high writing quality, many twists or a surprising ending. Dramatic irony reverses the direction of an existing information idea: the audience knows more than a character, while information withholding often means the viewer has not yet been told.

## Redundancy and conversion challenges

- **Moral descent is already broadly covered.** `tropes.fall_from_grace` explicitly includes lost moral standing. `redemption`, `transformation_arc` and antihero already exist. Use directional/role-bound subtypes, not another broad moral-descent score. Institutional corruption remains a different subject.
- **Tonal variation has an existing home.** The full definition already provides temporal segments for emotional and pacing changes. Add transition concepts and query rules instead of claiming every tonal-shift label requires a new whole-work scalar.
- **Chronology has existing coverage.** Anachronic Order, Flashback and In Medias Res mostly refine nonlinearity, multiple timelines and information withholding. Specific retrieval may use sparse variants; generic nonlinear scores should not be duplicated.
- **Moral roles need context.** A protagonist can be villainous, morally gray or conventionally heroic; an antagonist can be a person, institution, internal struggle or environment. A No Antagonist source that names a flood as antagonist shows why labels cannot be copied literally into a no-opposition boolean.
- **Relationship labels do not establish identity.** Platonic Life-Partners does not imply heterosexual orientation. Ambiguously Gay, shipping and fan interpretation do not establish explicit LGBTQ representation. Use explicit/attributed establishment rules already in the full design.
- **Averted/subverted is not positive presence.** Source text can list a trope to explain why it does not occur, why an expected payoff is misleading, or why it is deconstructed. The import crosswalk needs application status/polarity and evidence rather than `tag exists → trait high`.
- **Oblique labels are dangerous.** The Dragon can identify an antagonist’s lieutenant, not a reptilian creature; The Ghost can mean an unseen referenced character, not a supernatural spirit; Pet the Dog and Kick the Dog can concern characterization rather than actual animal interaction. Canonical concept identity and local explanation matter.
- **Plot Armor and Anyone Can Die are interpretive.** They should not become calibrated survival probabilities. Specific outcomes require character bindings, evidence and spoiler control.
- **Existing duplicates stay rejected.** Heroic Sacrifice supports the already proposed sacrifice theme; Dirty Cop supports proposed corruption; Running Gag, Unreliable Narrator, Love Triangle, Downer Ending and Bittersweet Ending already have direct equivalents.
