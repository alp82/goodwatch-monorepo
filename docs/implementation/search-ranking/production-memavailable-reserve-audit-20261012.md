# Audit: archive size is not an equal host-RAM commitment

Local source/evidence review for map 100, with historical reference to closed issue #406. No host operation, query, configuration change or guard change is authorized or performed by this packet. The existing 13 GiB candidate guard remains unchanged. The finding is that its archive term is a conservative all-copies-resident scenario, not a source-established additional nonreclaimable RAM requirement. Treating two complete on-disk archives as two mandatory allocations against MemAvailable can over-reserve reclaimable cache; replacing the whole guard with a 2 GiB floor is also not established by existing evidence.

## Linux accounting

Linux 6.8 defines MemAvailable as an estimate for new allocations without swap, incorporating file-LRU and reclaimable kernel memory as well as free pages. It reserves watermarks and discounts some cache/slab. It is neither MemFree nor a guarantee that a latency-sensitive reclaim will complete promptly. [Kernel proc documentation](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/v6.8/filesystems/proc.html).

The pinned implementation starts with free pages minus totalreserve_pages; adds active-file plus inactive-file pages minus min(half that cache, low-watermark pages); and adds reclaimable kernel pages with a similar discount. It does not subtract Dirty or Writeback separately. In particular it does not reserve half of every large archive: once the watermark is the smaller term, most file-LRU cache contributes to availability. [Linux 6.8 si_mem_available](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.8/mm/show_mem.c#L30-L66).

Consequently, creating a clean, ordinarily reclaimable archive cache page generally exchanges free memory for counted file-LRU memory; it does not permanently consume one additional byte of MemAvailable per byte of archive. Already-resident clean archive cache is already part of this estimate, rather than an extra anonymous allocation to subtract again. Future sequential copies can reclaim/reuse cache instead of requiring every byte of both complete files to stay resident. These are deductions from the formula, not a prediction of this server's instantaneous response. Low-watermark transitions, kernel metadata, charging and dirty states can change the result.

Mlock is different. Successfully locked file-backed pages become unevictable, including pages that were resident before locking. Their previous reclaimability is lost. Subtracting the full *additional* lock budget at host admission is conservative and appropriate; only counting initially missing pages would miss this resident-page conversion. The exact MemAvailable decrement is not guaranteed to equal the locked-byte count because the estimate and concurrent workloads change. This does not mean subtract an existing held budget again every steady pass. [Kernel unevictable-LRU documentation](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/v6.8/mm/unevictable-lru.html); [mlock/munlock manual](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/munlock.2.html).

Dirty or writeback pages are not the same as immediately reclaimable clean cache. Reclaim may defer them, activate them again, wait on completion or encounter IO stalls; MemAvailable's formula does not establish an immediate clean-page inventory. A network/FUSE stall can matter even while MemAvailable appears ample. [Linux 6.8 reclaim implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.8/mm/vmscan.c#L995-L1052).

## Snapshot bytes and their memory classes

The pinned Qdrant collection path creates a disk-backed temporary tar and shard-temporary directory, completes the archive and asks a storage manager to publish it. The manager hashes the file, moves it to destination temporary storage and publishes it; move_file first tries rename and falls back to copying. The observed different source/destination devices therefore justify disk-space/IO accounting for coexisting source and target archives. They do not prove both complete files must be resident or anonymous at once. The published target is the same destination file after rename, so there is no source-grounded third complete copy. [Collection snapshot](https://github.com/qdrant/qdrant/blob/v1.19.1/lib/collection/src/collection/snapshots.rs); [storage manager](https://github.com/qdrant/qdrant/blob/v1.19.1/lib/collection/src/common/snapshots_manager.rs); [move_file](https://github.com/qdrant/qdrant/blob/v1.19.1/lib/collection/src/common/file_utils.rs#L83-L110).

Segment snapshot_files appends vector/index/payload/id files into the archive. Conditional intermediary work remains; local-shard snapshots run segment work and WAL/clock handling, and snapshot proxification can create writable segments on normal segment storage. Nothing in this trace proves zero anonymous growth, zero dirty-file growth or a fixed 1 GiB staging bound. Nor does a regular file's apparent/allocated size measure its resident or dirty bytes. [Segment snapshot](https://github.com/qdrant/qdrant/blob/v1.19.1/lib/segment/src/segment/snapshot.rs#L232-L310); [local-shard snapshot](https://github.com/qdrant/qdrant/blob/v1.19.1/lib/collection/src/shards/local_shard/snapshot.rs).

The target uses SSHFS. Kernel FUSE supports direct and cached modes; cached writes may be write-through or writeback-cache. Buffers in the SSHFS/SSH userspace processes, socket/kernel memory, destination page cache and server-side storage/cache are different allocations. Existing source metadata does not establish all their modes or peak sizes. No claim that this SSHFS target is clean, directly reclaimable or bounded follows merely from a successful fsync/advice fixture. [FUSE IO modes](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/v6.8/filesystems/fuse-io.html).

Host and memcg gates remain distinct. memory.stat distinguishes anon, file, kernel, dirty/writeback, file-LRU and unevictable state; file includes tmpfs/shared memory and is not synonymous with reclaimable clean archives. A cgroup can hit its own cap with globally available memory. First-touch/source and helper charging, missing target pages, mapping overhead and ancestors still matter. Resident pages need not all be charged to the current helper/source cgroup. [Cgroup-v2 memory accounting](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/v6.8/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html#memory).

## Arithmetic, without changing a policy

The HEADROOM-PLAN candidate is 2×5GiB archives + 1 GiB staging + 2 GiB floor = 13 GiB, or 13,958,643,712B; adding the 1,725,227,008B full lock budget requires 15,683,870,720B. This arithmetic is correct for the declared worst-case reservation. Its interpretation as necessary additional host memory for clean archive copies is not justified by archive disk sizes.

| Illustrative host admission | Required bytes | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Existing candidate 13 GiB reserve + full new locks | 15,683,870,720 | Conservative policy; all archive bytes and staging are budgeted as if unavailable to other allocations |
| 2 GiB floor + full new locks only | 3,872,710,656 | Omits unknown transient nonreclaimable/dirty and latency costs; not a validated replacement |
| 2 GiB floor + full new locks + reviewed incremental transient allowance | 3,872,710,656 + allowance | Potential corrected model; allowance has not yet been measured or frozen |

At08:31:39 the observed 13,925,912,576B MemAvailable misses the existing candidate by 1,757,958,144B. Subtracting only full locks would leave 12,200,685,568B, and subtracting a 2 GiB floor as well leaves 10,053,201,920B for incremental transient use. That is arithmetic on an unpinned idle sample, not proof of admission or a safe future interval. The 2 GiB floor itself is a declared operational policy, not a Linux-provided safety theorem. IO/CPU/reclaim latency can invalidate the trial even when a corrected memory budget fits.

## What current observations can and cannot settle

PR469 retains 59 samples, two incomplete scans and early supervisor termination. It observed 8,003,628,096B concurrent archive-copy size on available samples, major-fault/read/reclaim activity and MemAvailable minimum 12,199,165,952B. These are aggregate background-plus-observer measurements, without pins and without clean full-window growth coverage. They do not prove archive bytes caused an equivalent MemAvailable loss or bound future dirty/anonymous peaks. The earlier complete baseline/new-inode positive growth also mixes normal turnover and sparse file allocation; retired files need not coexist. It cannot convert a 2.73 GB positive allocated-file delta into 2.73 GB mandatory RAM. Snapshot-stage growth remains UNKNOWN, but that means its *memory* allowance requires different evidence rather than mechanically reserving all on-disk growth.

A useful retained/adjusted guard needs source-aligned, time-bounded measurements of: host MemAvailable/MemFree/active-file/inactive-file/Mlocked/Unevictable/Dirty/Writeback; Qdrant and SSHFS/SSH/helper memory.current and anon/kernel/file/dirty/writeback/unevictable; actual snapshot phase/process and archive-overlap metadata; IO/CPU/memory PSI and stalls; and pinned-class residency plus faults/latency separately. Include normal writes/optimization, backup overlap, builds and page updates. Some metrics may remain unavailable; absence must remain explicit. Periodic sampling misses bursts, and process attribution is not automatically causal.

Retaining a large archive-sized reserve can be justified as an explicitly chosen cache-working-set/IO performance allowance if measurements show dirty backlog, FUSE buffering, nonreclaimable growth or unacceptable reclaim latency requires that room. It must be labelled such, rather than a physical necessity proved by two files. Correcting it would require independently reviewed incremental transient bounds and abort conditions, a 2 GiB emergency policy, full-lock admission accounting, source/cgroup fences and controlled lock/release/pressure validation. Releasing pins on a low-memory/pressure guard is a bounded rollback, not a promise to prevent every inter-sample burst. No current policy change, relocation, production pin or capacity decision follows from this review.
