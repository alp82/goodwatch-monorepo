# TV controls and details, pass 1

Implemented October 20, 2026, on `feat/shared-scoring`, uncommitted. This is the approved first pass; the signed-in home redesign and Discover/search controls remain later passes.

## Behavior and evidence

The browser reproduction used the real TV with controlled catalog-shaped lists alternating short, long, and multiline title names. The starting implementation was also built from the captured files and driven on port 3420, including pointer focus, remote direction, remote wheel, keyboard arrows, and held keyboard input. This establishes runtime evidence rather than inferring a cause from source alone.

- Before: the desktop row’s top varied from 298.84 to 321.08 screen pixels as the focused name changed. Scrolling began at the sixth poster of a 12-poster row. The auto-sized `min-h-full` list grew with its heading.
- After: desktop row top is constant across those names. Heading/metadata and poster regions have reserved space. Desktop posters are 163 × 244 canvas pixels (previously 172 × 258); phone poster sizing stays the same.
- A safe region of 28% of row width, bounded by the item width, starts horizontal movement before the edge and keeps adjacent posters visible. Movement retargets from its current position and lasts at most 120 ms; held keyboard repeats are limited to one move per 120 ms. Reduced motion is instant. Direction measurements no longer snap a moving row to its destination.
- Recent TV history entries retain row offsets. Restoration waits for the actual posters, cancels pending animation, and restores the previous focus. Tested through title details and Back, with an exact row-offset assertion.
- Title controls retain the cinematic background and large title. Watch on…, Trailer, Want to See, Seen/rating, and Fingerprint are accessible in two rows on desktop and three on phone. Long details can scroll internally; focusing an action brings it into view. Runtime is also visible on phone.
- `/api/tv-title` reads the same country-specific title details as the title page. Only actual catalog HTTP(S) offer URLs can become Watch on… links. Subscription/free/ad-supported offers are eligible, owned services first; a rental never becomes a subscription action. Missing URLs lead to the explicitly labelled Where to watch fallback. Provider URLs are native `_blank` anchors, retain offer/country analytics attributes, and never write watch state.
- Trailer and Fingerprint are lazy title panels represented by compatible TV query parameters. Back and Close restore the invoking action, including deep-link fallback. Trailer reuses the existing YouTube player and first trailer selection.
- Fingerprint uses the actual title fingerprint, shared `composeHeadline`, area colors/strip computation, and `readScale` labels. Desktop shows all Feel/Story/Look readings. Phone has Summary and individual groups. These are descriptions and read-only bars, with no slider inputs or editable affordances. Missing data and loading failures say so.

The eXistenZ DE payload was read from the live local service for screenshots and replayed in the browser tests; its card includes the actual poster, backdrop, runtime, and score. Provider popups were intercepted after requesting the exact catalog URL, so no provider account or payment was involved. Browser tests assert pointer, Enter, and remote OK open separate tabs, keep the TV in place, and send no watch/tracking mutation. Trailer verification checks its iframe/video key, not successful playback from YouTube.

The corrected audit includes a TypeScript AST scan of string/template literals and JSX text (`tonight-literals-after-fixes.txt`), source search, and public-file search. Remaining source occurrences are compatible route/API/cache/DOM/MCP identities and developer comments/errors. Public sitemap occurrences name actual catalog titles, such as The Tonight Show, and are preserved. Historical `prototype-*` experiments are excluded from production by Vite and retained as historical artifacts, including their old copy.

Terminology ambiguity: Watch next previously described the top of a Wishlist view, while the TV’s Tonight could also continue an episode or fall back to discovery. `CONTEXT.md` explicitly records both uses under the new shared name, preserving each surface’s existing rules. No behavioral decision is implied by the name change.
