ORDINAL ASSESSMENT CLARIFICATION

The vector is a set of rubric-based judgments about a work, not a claim that every integer is an independently verified fact. Use your knowledge of the identified work together with the supplied synopsis. Direct viewing, external citations, exact scene counts and certainty about the precise integer are not prerequisites for a defensible ordinal judgment.

Assess each dimension independently. If you know the work well enough to judge its mood, themes, narrative or treatment, select the best-fitting integer under that dimension's rubric. Uncertainty between nearby scores is ordinary judgment uncertainty, not by itself a reason for null. Lack of knowledge about one dimension must not spread to dimensions you can assess.

Use null only where you genuinely lack enough knowledge to make that particular judgment. Do not invent details, force completeness, substitute zero for uncertainty, or assign trace points to merely imaginable associations. A defensible zero means the defined property has no assessed strength/importance under its rubric. Presence remains a separate occurrence judgment: centrality zero may coexist with present, and absence may not coexist with positive centrality. No target distribution or title-specific score is prescribed.

An unspecified edition or cut does not make the identified work wholly unknown. Assess stable work-level traits for the supplied adaptation and scope; abstain only on dimensions whose judgment genuinely depends on an unknown edition difference. Do not invent a cut or claim all editions are identical.
